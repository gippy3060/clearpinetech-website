How much does IT support cost for a physio, chiro or counselling clinic in BC?

Allied health · 30 October 2026

If you run a physiotherapy, chiropractic, massage therapy, naturopathic or counselling clinic in BC, you have probably noticed that IT quotes are hard to compare. This guide gives you the arithmetic for our own pricing, what a monthly fee should and should not cover, and the questions to ask any provider. Clearpine is a small Abbotsford-based managed IT provider, so we will be clear about where our pricing applies.

Our pricing, stated plainly

Clearpine’s managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. Support is delivered remotely during business hours. A few things are billed separately: Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits. The exception is that on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely.

“User” means a person who logs in to your clinic’s computers or accounts. In an allied health clinic that usually includes the practitioners, front-desk staff and the clinic manager. If you host independent practitioners who share your network, count them too.

What a monthly fee should cover

Ask any provider to put this in writing. Two quotes that look 30 per cent apart can cover very different work.

  • Help desk. Who answers when a practitioner cannot open the schedule or a treatment room computer will not start, and during what hours?
  • Patching and monitoring. Are computers, the router or firewall and any server updated and watched, or only fixed after they break?
  • Backups. Are your charting and billing data, scanned intake forms and shared drives backed up off site, and has a restore ever been tested?
  • Security. Multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection and email filtering are the baseline now.
  • Vendor liaison. Will the provider talk to your booking, charting and billing software vendors so you are not the go-between?
  • Documentation. Will you receive an inventory of devices and accounts that you can reach if you change providers?

What is usually billed on top

  • Microsoft 365 licences for email and documents, billed per user. With us these are billed separately from the $95 support rate.
  • Clinic software. Scheduling, charting, billing and virtual care platforms are licensed by their vendors, not by your IT provider.
  • Hardware such as workstations, laptops or tablets for treatment rooms, a firewall, Wi-Fi access points, phones and card terminals.
  • After-hours work. Moving a clinic, replacing equipment or changing the network is often best done outside appointment hours. With us, after-hours support is billed separately.
  • Projects such as opening a new clinic, a move or a hardware refresh.

Remote support and the on-site question

Most clinic IT problems are not physical: a failed login, a stuck print job, a slow computer or a backup warning. These can be fixed remotely, which is why remote support is the base of our service and keeps the monthly figure down. Some problems are physical, such as a dead switch, a failed drive or a computer that will not power on. For those, on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely. Planned on-site visits you ask for are billed separately.

Our free IT assessment can be done on site at any location within 300 km of Abbotsford, which includes Surrey, Langley, Vancouver and the rest of the Lower Mainland, or remotely.

Why allied health clinics are different from other small offices

Shared spaces and shared devices

Many clinics rent rooms to independent practitioners, share a front desk and let patients use a waiting-room Wi-Fi network. Without separation, a patient’s phone and a practitioner’s notes can sit on the same network. A good provider will ask who uses what and set up separate staff and guest networks.

Confidential notes

Counsellors and psychologists keep particularly sensitive notes, and every practitioner type keeps health information. Private-sector clinics in BC are generally subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), and your regulatory college sets its own record-keeping expectations. Your college, association and legal counsel can confirm what applies to you. The practical IT safeguards are access controls, multi-factor authentication, encrypted laptops and tablets, screen locks and backups that ransomware cannot encrypt. See PIPEDA for clinics: what your IT actually has to do.

Virtual care

If you offer video sessions, you need a private room, a reliable connection and a platform that your profession and your privacy officer are comfortable with. This is a policy decision as much as a technical one, and a provider can help you carry it out.

Downtime has a direct cost

When the schedule is down, appointments are missed or delayed. Think about what an hour of lost treatment time costs, then compare that to the monthly fee. That is usually a better guide than the fee alone.

Questions to ask any IT provider

  1. What is the price per user, per device or flat, and what is the minimum term?
  2. Which items are billed separately: licences, after-hours work, on-site visits, projects?
  3. Have you supported clinics, and will you work with my booking, charting and billing vendors?
  4. How are backups tested, and when did you last test-restore one?
  5. How do you handle independent practitioners who use their own devices?
  6. What happens if I leave: who owns the documentation and admin accounts?
  7. Can you do the initial assessment on site at no charge?

In-house, break-fix or managed?

Almost no allied health clinic is large enough to employ IT staff. Break-fix has no monthly fee, but problems are often found when a patient is already waiting. A managed plan swaps the unpredictable invoice for a known monthly cost and proactive maintenance. Our guide to managed IT support vs hiring in-house compares the models in more detail.

How to compare quotes fairly

Put each quote on the same basis: a monthly cost per user, plus whatever the provider bills separately, such as licences, after-hours work and on-site visits. A low headline rate that excludes backups, security tools or on-site help can cost more than a higher rate that includes them. Also check the contract term, how to leave, and who holds the administrator accounts.
