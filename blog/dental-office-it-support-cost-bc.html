How much does IT support cost for a dental office in BC?

Dental IT · 28 October 2026

If you run a dental practice in BC, you have probably asked a simple question and received a vague answer: what should IT support actually cost? This guide gives you the arithmetic for our own pricing, a checklist of what is and is not included, and the questions to put to any provider you compare us with. Clearpine is a small Abbotsford-based managed IT provider, so we will be clear about where our pricing applies and where it does not.

Our pricing, stated plainly

Clearpine’s managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. Support is delivered remotely during business hours. A few things are billed separately: Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits. The exception is that on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely.

“User” means a person who uses a computer or account in your practice, not a chair. In a dental office that usually includes the dentists, hygienists, assistants who log in to charting or imaging, front-desk staff and the office manager.

What a monthly fee should cover

Whoever you hire, ask them to put this in writing. Price alone tells you very little, because two quotes that look 30 per cent apart can cover very different work.

  • Help desk. Who answers when the front desk cannot print a treatment plan or log in to the schedule, and during what hours?
  • Patching and monitoring. Are workstations, the server and the firewall updated and watched, or only fixed when something breaks?
  • Backups. Is your practice-management database, your imaging archive and your document storage backed up, kept off site, and test-restored?
  • Security. Multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection and email filtering are the baseline now, not extras.
  • Vendor liaison. Will the provider talk to your practice-software and imaging vendors so you do not become the go-between?
  • Documentation. Will you receive an inventory of devices, accounts and passwords stored somewhere you can reach if the provider changes?

What is usually billed on top

The cost of IT for a dental office is more than the support fee. Plan for these separately:

  • Microsoft 365 licences for email and documents, billed per user. With us these are billed separately from the $95 support rate.
  • Practice-management and imaging software licences and support contracts from your software vendors.
  • Hardware such as workstations, operatory computers, the firewall, switches and a server where your software needs one. Imaging workstations often carry vendor specifications.
  • After-hours work. Moving an office, replacing a server or changing the network is often best done on a weekend so no patients are affected. With us, after-hours support is billed separately.
  • Projects such as a new-office build, a relocation or a software migration.

Remote support and the on-site question

Most dental IT problems are not physical: a login that fails, a printer queue that jams, a slow workstation, a failed update or a backup warning. These can be fixed remotely, which is why remote support is the base of our service and keeps the monthly figure down. Some problems are physical, such as a dead switch, a failed drive or an operatory computer that will not power up. For those, on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely. Planned on-site visits you ask for are billed separately.

Our free IT assessment can be done on site at any location within 300 km of Abbotsford, which covers Surrey, Langley, Vancouver and the rest of the Lower Mainland, or remotely.

Why dental offices are different from other small offices

A dental office looks like a small business, but its IT has a few demands that general office support often overlooks.

Imaging and storage

Digital X-ray sensors, panoramic units and CBCT machines produce large files and depend on specific drivers, software versions and storage permissions. A provider who updates a workstation without checking the imaging vendor’s requirements can stop a sensor from working in the middle of a clinic day. Ask any provider how they handle changes to imaging computers.

Patient records and privacy

Private dental practices in BC are generally subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), and the College of Dental Surgeons of BC sets professional expectations for patient records. Your college, professional association and legal counsel can confirm exactly what applies to you. From an IT standpoint, the practical safeguards are access controls, multi-factor authentication, encryption on laptops, and backups that cannot be encrypted by ransomware. For more detail, read PIPEDA for clinics: what your IT actually has to do.

Downtime has a direct cost

If the schedule and charts are down, hygiene appointments stop and patients wait. Think about what an hour of downtime costs your practice in lost chair time and rework, then compare that with the monthly fee. That comparison is usually a better guide than the fee alone.

Questions to ask any IT provider

  1. What is the price per user, per device or flat, and what is the minimum term?
  2. Which items are billed separately: licences, after-hours work, on-site visits, projects?
  3. Do you have experience with dental practice software and imaging equipment, and will you work with my vendors?
  4. How are backups tested, and when did you last test-restore one?
  5. What happens if I leave: who owns the documentation and admin accounts?
  6. Can you do the initial assessment on site at no charge?

In-house, break-fix or managed?

Most single-location dental offices are too small to justify a full-time IT employee. A break-fix arrangement, where you call someone when something fails, has no monthly fee but often means the problems are found only when patients are in the chair. A managed plan swaps the unpredictable invoice for a known monthly cost and proactive maintenance. We compare the models in more detail in managed IT support vs hiring in-house.

How to compare quotes fairly

When you collect two or three quotes, put them side by side on the same basis. Convert everything to a monthly cost per user, then add the items each provider bills separately, such as licences, after-hours work and on-site visits. A low headline rate that excludes backups, security tools or on-site help can end up costing more than a higher rate that includes them. Also check the contract term, how to leave, and who holds the administrator accounts. A provider who is confident in their service should be happy to explain all of this before you sign anything.
