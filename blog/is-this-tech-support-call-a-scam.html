How to Tell if a Tech Support Call Is a Scam

Your phone rings. A friendly voice says your computer has a virus, or that someone has used your account. Or a loud message fills your screen with a phone number to call. What do you do?

This guide is written in plain words. You can read it slowly, and you can share it with someone you love. The short version: a real company does not phone you out of the blue to say your computer is broken. If that is what is happening, it is almost certainly a scam.

If it is happening right now

  1. Do not give them anything. No passwords, no card numbers, no codes sent to your phone.
  2. Do not let them into your computer. If they asked you to type an address, install a program or "press a key", stop.
  3. Hang up. You do not have to be polite. Hanging up is safe.
  4. If a pop-up will not close, hold the power button until the computer switches off. Switch it on again. Do not call the number on the pop-up.
  5. Tell someone. A family member, a friend or your bank. Scammers count on you keeping it secret.

Seven warning signs

  1. They called, texted or emailed you first. Microsoft, Apple, your internet provider and your bank do not contact you to say your computer has a problem.
  2. A pop-up shows a phone number. Real warnings from your computer do not tell you to call a number, and they do not lock your screen.
  3. They say it is urgent. "Act now or you will lose everything" is pressure, not information. An honest helper lets you take your time.
  4. They ask you to keep it secret. "Don't tell the bank" or "don't tell your family" is a very bad sign.
  5. They want you to install a program or let them control your computer when you did not ask for help. Remote help is safe only when you chose to ask for it, and you can end it at any time.
  6. They ask for gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency. No honest business asks for these. Normal businesses take an ordinary card payment.
  7. They say they will send you a refund and then say they sent too much. Then they ask you to send money back. This is a known trick.

What if I already let them in, or paid?

It happens to careful people. It is not your fault, and there is a lot you can do.

  1. Disconnect. Turn off the Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable, then switch the computer off.
  2. Call your bank or card company at once if you gave card or bank details or sent money. Use the number on the back of your card. Ask them to stop or reverse payments if they can.
  3. Change your passwords from a different, safe device such as a family member's phone. Start with email and banking.
  4. Report it. In Canada: the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. In the USA: ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Reports help investigators, even when money cannot be recovered.
  5. Have the computer checked. Scammers sometimes leave remote-access software behind. We can look for it and remove it. See virus, pop-up and scam clean-up. We cannot get lost money back; your bank is the right place for that.

How do I know a helper is honest?

  • You phoned them, not the other way round.
  • They tell you the price before they start. Ours is $100 CAD flat, with no charge if we can't fix the problem remotely.
  • They ask your permission before they do anything, and you can end the session at any time.
  • They never ask for gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency.
  • They are happy to let a family member listen in. You can ask the same question twice.

You do not have to choose us. If you would rather get help from someone you know, or from the company that made your device, that is a good choice too.

Common questions

Is it really that rare for Microsoft or Apple to call me? Yes. They do not make unrequested calls about viruses on your computer.

The caller knew my name and address. Does that make them real? No. Scammers buy lists of names and addresses, or find them online.

The caller ID shows my bank's name. Caller ID can be faked. Hang up and phone the number printed on your card or statement.

Can I just ignore the pop-up? Usually yes. Close the browser, or switch the computer off and on again. If it comes back, call us or ask someone you trust to look.
