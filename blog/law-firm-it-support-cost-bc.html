How much does IT support cost for a law firm in BC?

Law firms · 29 October 2026

A lawyer choosing IT support usually gets quotes that are hard to compare: per device, per user, flat fee, hourly. This guide sets out what managed IT costs for a small BC law firm using our own pricing, what is billed separately, and what a firm handling client confidences and trust funds should insist on in writing. Clearpine is a small Abbotsford-based managed IT provider, so we are specific about what applies to us. This is general information, not legal advice.

Our pricing, stated plainly

Clearpine’s managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. Support is delivered remotely during business hours. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, except that on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely.

In a law firm, “user” means each person with their own login: lawyers, paralegals, legal assistants, bookkeepers and the office manager.

What a monthly fee should cover for a law firm

Ask any provider to confirm each of these in writing. Two quotes that look 30 per cent apart can cover very different work.

  • Help desk. Who answers when a lawyer cannot open a document or join a hearing, and during what hours?
  • Patching and monitoring. Workstations, laptops, the firewall and any server kept current and watched, not fixed only after failure.
  • Backups that are tested. Client files, practice-management data and accounting records backed up off site, with test restores. See why backups quietly fail.
  • Email security. Multi-factor authentication, phishing filtering and protection against spoofed payment-change emails.
  • Device security. Encrypted laptops and phones, so a lost device is an inconvenience rather than a confidentiality problem.
  • Documentation. An inventory of devices, accounts and admin access that you hold, not only the provider.

What is usually billed on top

  • Microsoft 365 licences, billed per user. With us these are separate from the $95 support rate.
  • Practice-management and accounting software such as Clio, PCLaw, Actionstep or LEAP, which you license from the vendor. We do not resell them.
  • Hardware such as laptops, docking stations, scanners, the firewall and a server if you still run one.
  • After-hours work. A move, a migration or a network change is often best done on a weekend. With us, after-hours support is billed separately.
  • Projects such as setting up a new firm or moving to the cloud.

Remote support and on-site visits

Most legal IT problems are not physical: a login, a mailbox rule, a slow laptop, a printer or a remote-access issue. Remote support handles them quickly and keeps the monthly rate down. For hardware that has genuinely failed, on-site visits are free if the problem can’t be fixed remotely. Our free IT assessment can be done on site at any location within 300 km of Abbotsford, which includes Vancouver, Surrey, Langley, Burnaby and the rest of the Lower Mainland, or remotely.

Why law firms are different from other small offices

Confidentiality is a professional duty

The Law Society of BC’s Code of Professional Conduct requires lawyers to protect client confidentiality, and the Law Society publishes guidance on using cloud services. An IT provider cannot take that duty from you, but it can help you show reasonable care: access controls, encryption, multi-factor authentication and a record of the due diligence you did on vendors. Confirm current requirements with the Law Society and your professional liability insurer. Our guide to vetting Clio and other cloud practice software sets out the questions to ask.

Trust accounting and payment fraud

Law firms are targeted by wire and payment-redirection fraud because they handle large sums. Good IT support limits who can approve trust transactions, protects the devices used for online banking and filters impersonation emails. It does not replace the call-back-to-a-known-number habit, which remains the best defence.

Deadlines make downtime expensive

A court filing deadline or a closing does not move because the server failed. Compare the monthly fee with what a day of lost billable time and missed deadlines would cost your firm. That is a better yardstick than the fee alone.

Questions to ask any IT provider

  1. What is the price per user, per device or flat, and what is the minimum term?
  2. Which items are billed separately: licences, after-hours work, on-site visits, projects?
  3. How do you handle client confidentiality, and will you sign a confidentiality agreement?
  4. When did you last test-restore a backup for a client, and how do you prove it?
  5. Will you work with my practice-management vendor when something breaks?
  6. What happens if I leave: who holds the documentation and admin accounts?
  7. Can you do the initial assessment on site at no charge?

Managed, break-fix or in-house?

Firms under about 20 people rarely justify a full-time IT employee. Break-fix has no monthly fee but means problems are found when a lawyer is already stuck. Managed support turns an unpredictable invoice into a known monthly cost with proactive maintenance. We compare the models in managed IT support vs hiring in-house.

How to compare quotes fairly

Put every quote on the same basis. Convert each to a monthly cost per user, then add what that provider bills separately: licences, after-hours work, on-site visits and any set-up fee. A low headline rate that leaves out backups, email security or on-site help can end up costing more than a higher rate that includes them. Check the contract term, the notice needed to leave, and who holds the administrator accounts and passwords. Also ask whether the person who answers the phone is the person who will visit your office, because continuity matters in a firm where staff handle sensitive files every day.

Finally, ask for a sample of what a typical month looks like: which updates were applied, which backups were tested and which tickets were closed. A provider that reports on its work is easier to hold to account than one that only invoices.
