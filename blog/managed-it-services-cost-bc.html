How Much Do Managed IT Services Cost in BC?

Local business · 27 November 2026

If you run a small business in the Lower Mainland or Fraser Valley and you're shopping for IT support, the first question is usually "what will this cost per month?" Most providers price managed IT per user, but the number on the website rarely tells the whole story. This guide covers the price ranges BC providers publish, what those prices usually include and leave out, and how to compare quotes fairly.

The short answer: typical per-user prices

When we reviewed the websites of managed IT providers around the Lower Mainland in October 2026, the ones that published prices fell into roughly these bands:

  • About $70–$100 per user per month for entry or core plans: help desk, monitoring, patching and basic security.
  • About $100–$150 per user per month for fuller plans that add stronger security tools, backup and more proactive work.
  • $150–$200+ per user per month for premium or compliance-heavy plans, including healthcare and finance work, or plans with on-site time built in.

Many providers don't publish a price at all and quote after a discovery call. Prices change, so treat these as a starting point and confirm with each provider.

What a managed IT price usually includes

A per-user plan should cover the day-to-day running of your IT. In most plans that means:

  • Remote help desk for staff during business hours
  • Monitoring and patching of computers and, where relevant, servers
  • Security basics: endpoint protection, multi-factor authentication, email filtering
  • Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace administration (not the licences themselves)
  • User setup and removal when people join or leave
  • Network and Wi-Fi support for your office
  • Backup monitoring, and ideally regular restore tests

What is often extra

This is where two quotes that look similar can end up hundreds of dollars apart each month. Ask about each of these:

  • Software licences. Microsoft 365, Google Workspace and line-of-business software are almost always billed separately.
  • After-hours support. Evenings, weekends and holidays are commonly billed by the hour.
  • On-site visits. Some plans include them, some charge per visit or per hour plus travel. In a spread-out region, travel matters. Our guide to onsite IT support costs in the Fraser Valley covers this in more detail.
  • Onboarding fees. A one-time charge to document your systems and install tools.
  • Projects. Office moves, server replacements, migrations and new hardware are usually quoted separately.
  • Hardware. Laptops, firewalls and Wi-Fi equipment are normally purchased on top.
  • Contract terms. Minimum user counts, term length and what happens if you leave early.

Managed IT vs. the alternatives

Break-fix (pay per call). Cheap in a quiet month and expensive in a bad one, with no one watching for problems in between. It can suit very small offices with simple setups.

An in-house IT person. Worth it once you have enough work to keep someone busy full time, but one person can't cover every skill, holiday and sick day. We compare the two in in-house IT vs managed services.

A solo contractor. Often good value and personal, but you depend on one person's availability. See independent contractor vs MSP.

Why prices are higher for clinics and law firms

Healthcare and legal practices handle sensitive personal information under BC privacy law and their own regulators' rules. That usually means stronger security tools, more careful backup and documentation, and practice-software support, which pushes prices toward the top of the range. We cover those costs in detail for dental offices, law firms, allied health clinics and veterinary clinics.

How to compare two quotes

  1. Convert each quote to the same thing: total monthly cost for your real number of users, including licences.
  2. Get the exclusions list in writing.
  3. Ask for written response times, not "we're usually quick".
  4. Ask how backups are tested and how often.
  5. Ask who comes on site, from where, and what it costs.
  6. Check the contract term, notice period and how you get your data and passwords back if you leave.

Frequently asked questions

How much do managed IT services cost per user in BC?

Published per-user prices from Lower Mainland managed IT providers in late 2026 mostly fall between about $70 and $200 per user per month, with many core plans between $80 and $150. Regulated work such as healthcare or legal compliance is often priced higher. Clearpine charges $95 per user per month, minimum 5 users, on a 1-year agreement.

What is usually not included in a managed IT price?

Common extras are Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace licences, hardware, after-hours support, on-site visits, onboarding fees and projects such as office moves or server replacements. Ask every provider for a written list of exclusions before comparing quotes.

Is managed IT cheaper than hiring an IT person?

For most businesses under about 50 staff, yes. A full-time IT employee costs a salary plus benefits and still needs cover for holidays and sick days, while managed IT for 10 to 20 users usually costs a fraction of that per year and includes a team, tools and monitoring.

Do managed IT providers charge onboarding fees?

Some do and some don't. Onboarding covers documenting your systems, installing monitoring and security tools and fixing urgent gaps. Ask whether it is a one-time fee, included, or billed hourly, and what it covers.
