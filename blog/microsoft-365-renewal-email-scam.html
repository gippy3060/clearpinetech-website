Is That Microsoft 365 Renewal Email a Scam? How to Tell

Staying safe · 12 November 2026

An email or text says your Microsoft 365 subscription has renewed, often for a few hundred dollars, and gives a phone number "to cancel or get a refund". Or it says your payment failed and your files will be deleted. Please don't call the number or click the links. These messages are one of the most common scams aimed at home computer users.

How the scam works

The scammers want you to phone them. When you call, a friendly "agent" offers a refund, then asks you to install a program so they can "process" it. That program gives them control of your computer. They then fake a large refund, claim they sent you too much, and pressure you to send the difference by gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency, or they log in to your online banking.

Warning signs

  • A phone number to call to cancel or get a refund.
  • A large amount you don't remember agreeing to.
  • Urgent language: "within 24 hours", "your account will be suspended", "files will be deleted".
  • An attachment, often a PDF "invoice".
  • A sender address that is not from Microsoft, or a greeting like "Dear Customer". (Sender names can be faked, so a normal-looking sender does not prove it is real.)
  • Anyone asking for payment by gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency. A real company does not do this.

How to check for real, in two minutes

  1. Close the email. Don't reply.
  2. Open your web browser and type account.microsoft.com yourself.
  3. Sign in, then look at Services & subscriptions and Order history.
  4. Check your bank or credit card statement for the charge.

If there is no matching charge, the email was fake. Delete it. If there is a real renewal you don't want, you can turn off automatic renewal yourself on that same page. You don't need to phone anyone to do it.

Already called, or let someone onto your computer?

Don't be embarrassed. These scams fool careful people every day. Act calmly but soon:

  1. Hang up and turn off the computer's internet (unplug the cable or switch off Wi-Fi) if they are still connected.
  2. Call your bank using the number on the back of your card. Tell them what happened, especially if you logged in to online banking while the caller was connected.
  3. Change your passwords from a different, safe device, starting with email and banking.
  4. Have the computer checked for remote-access programs the caller left behind. See virus, pop-up and scam clean-up.
  5. Report it. In Canada: the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. In the USA: ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

More warning signs are in our article How to Tell if a Tech Support Call Is a Scam.
