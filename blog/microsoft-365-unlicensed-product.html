Word or Excel Says "Unlicensed Product"? How to Fix It

You open Word or Excel and the top of the window says "Unlicensed Product". You can read your documents, but you can't type, edit or save. It is worrying, but your files are almost always safe. The app simply can't confirm that you have paid for it.

Here are the usual reasons, and what to check for each one. Go slowly. Looking at these settings can't harm anything.

Step 1: See which account is signed in

  1. Open Word.
  2. Click File at the top left, then Account at the bottom of the list. On a Mac, look at the top right of the Word window for your name or picture, and click it to see the account.
  3. Look at the email address shown. Is it the one you used to buy Microsoft 365 or Office?

Many people have more than one Microsoft account without knowing it, for example an old Hotmail address and a newer Outlook.com one. If the wrong one is signed in, sign out, then sign in with the account that paid.

Step 2: Check that the subscription is still active

  1. Type account.microsoft.com into your web browser yourself. Don't use a search ad or a link in an email.
  2. Sign in with your Microsoft account.
  3. Choose Services & subscriptions.

If Microsoft 365 shows as expired, the card on file may have run out or been replaced. You can update it there, or decide not to renew. If nothing is listed at all, try the other Microsoft accounts you might have.

If you share someone's Microsoft 365 Family plan, the person who pays can check under Sharing that you are still included.

Step 3: Look for two copies of Office

New computers often come with a trial copy of Office. If you then install a copy you bought, the two can confuse each other.

  1. On Windows, open Start, then Settings, then Apps, then Installed apps.
  2. Type Office or Microsoft 365 in the search box.
  3. If you see more than one entry, don't remove anything yet. Note what you see, or phone us. Removing the wrong one is a common mistake.

Step 4: Check your device limit

Microsoft 365 Personal and Family let each person stay signed in on several devices at the same time, but there is a limit. If you have signed in on old computers you no longer use, sign them out. You can see and manage this at account.microsoft.com under Services & subscriptions.

Step 5: Restart and sign in again

Close all Office apps, restart the computer, open Word and sign in again when asked. This clears a surprising number of license problems, especially after a Windows update or a password change.

Bought Office once, with a product key?

One-time purchases such as Office Home are linked to the Microsoft account used when the product key was first entered. Sign in with that account. If you are not sure which one it was, we can help you look.

A warning: many websites copy the look of Microsoft and offer to "activate Office" for a fee, or sell very cheap product keys. Microsoft's own pages end in microsoft.com or office.com. If a website asks you to call a number to activate Office, close it.

Don't need all of Office?

Microsoft offers free web versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint that you use in a web browser with a free Microsoft account. They have fewer features, but for letters and simple lists they may be all you need. You don't have to pay for a subscription just to open your own documents.
