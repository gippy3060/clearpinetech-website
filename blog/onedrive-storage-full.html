OneDrive or Outlook Storage Full? What to Do

A message says your OneDrive storage is full, or your Microsoft storage is "almost full". It can come with a warning that email will stop working. This article explains why it happens and how to free space without losing your photos and files.

First, check the message is real. Scammers send fake "storage full" emails with links to fake payment pages. Don't click links in the email. Type account.microsoft.com into your browser yourself and look at your storage there. If it is fine there, the email was fake.

Why it fills up

  • A free Microsoft account has a small amount of storage. At the time of writing it is 5 GB, shared between OneDrive files and Outlook.com email attachments. Paid plans give much more.
  • OneDrive may be backing up your whole computer. Windows often offers to back up your Desktop, Documents and Pictures folders to OneDrive. Many people said yes without realising it. Years of photos can fill a free account quickly.
  • Photos from your phone. The OneDrive app on a phone can upload every photo you take.
  • The OneDrive recycle bin. Deleted files stay there for a while and still count towards your storage.
  • Large email attachments that you never deleted.

What happens when it is full

  • New files and photos stop going to OneDrive, so they are not backed up.
  • On a free account, Microsoft warns that you may not be able to send or receive Outlook.com email until you free up space or add storage.
  • Your existing files are not deleted straight away. You have time to sort it out calmly.

Before you delete anything: one important warning

If OneDrive is syncing a folder, deleting a file on your computer also deletes it from OneDrive, and deleting it from OneDrive also deletes it from your computer. This is the most common way people lose photos while "freeing up space". If you want to keep a file but not in OneDrive, copy it to a USB drive or external drive first, and check the copy opens.

Safe ways to free up space

  1. See what is using the space. Sign in at account.microsoft.com, or at onedrive.com, and open the storage page. It shows how much is files and how much is email.
  2. Empty the OneDrive recycle bin. On onedrive.com, open Recycle bin. Look through it first, then empty it if nothing there is needed.
  3. Clear out big email attachments. In Outlook.com, delete old emails with large attachments you no longer need, then empty the Deleted Items folder.
  4. Move large files you want to keep to an external drive. Videos are usually the biggest. Copy first, check, and only then delete.
  5. Decide about computer folder backup. In the OneDrive settings on your computer, under backup, you can see which folders are being backed up. Stopping it can make files look like they vanished from your Desktop or Documents; they are still in OneDrive. If you are unsure, ask for help before changing this.

Or add more storage

Microsoft sells plans with more storage, including Microsoft 365 Basic and the Personal and Family plans, which also include Word and Excel. Check current options and prices on Microsoft's own website. Paying is a choice, not a must. Sometimes a tidy-up plus an external drive is enough.
