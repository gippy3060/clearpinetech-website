Why Is My Wi-Fi Slow Upstairs? Simple Fixes

Wi-Fi · 9 November 2026

Wi-Fi works fine in the kitchen. Upstairs, videos stop, pages crawl and calls drop. This is very common, and the cause is usually where the Wi-Fi box sits, not your internet plan. Try these steps in order. You cannot harm anything by trying them.

Why Wi-Fi gets weaker upstairs

Wi-Fi is a radio signal. It gets weaker with distance and when it has to pass through floors, walls, brick, concrete, metal and even a fridge or a mirror. Many homes have the router in a basement, a closet or a corner, so the signal has a long, hard trip to reach a bedroom upstairs.

Step 1: Restart the Wi-Fi box

  1. Unplug the router (the box from your internet company) from the power for 30 seconds.
  2. Plug it back in and wait a few minutes for the lights to settle.
  3. Try again upstairs.

Step 2: Check whether it is the Wi-Fi or the internet

  • Stand next to the router and test a video or a web page. If it is also slow there, the problem is probably the internet service, not the Wi-Fi, and your internet company is the right place to ask.
  • If it is fast next to the router but slow upstairs, the problem is the Wi-Fi signal. Keep reading.

Step 3: Move the router

  • Place it in the open, up off the floor, and as central in the house as you can. A shelf in a main room is better than a cupboard.
  • Keep it away from the microwave, cordless phone bases, thick walls and large metal objects.
  • If the internet cable comes in at a fixed spot, you may not be able to move it far. That is a good reason to try Step 5.

Step 4: Try the other Wi-Fi band

Most routers send two kinds of signal, often called 2.4 and 5. The 5 signal is faster but does not travel as far. The 2.4 signal is slower but reaches further. If your phone or laptop shows two networks with similar names, try the one with 2.4 in the name when you are upstairs. If you only see one name, your router may be combining them, which is fine.

Step 5: Add a second Wi-Fi point

If one box cannot reach the whole house, a second one helps. There are two common choices:

  • A Wi-Fi extender repeats the signal. It is the cheaper option, but it can only repeat what it receives, so it must sit about halfway, where the signal is still good, not in the dead spot.
  • A mesh system is a set of two or three matching boxes that work as one network. It costs more but usually gives steadier Wi-Fi in a larger or two-storey home.

A wire (network cable) to the second box gives the best result, if one can be run.

Other things that slow Wi-Fi down

  • Many devices using the Wi-Fi at once, such as video calls and streaming in several rooms.
  • An old router. Routers older than about five or six years often cannot keep up with today's phones and TVs.
  • Wi-Fi from a neighbour's router crowding the same channel in an apartment or townhouse.
  • Out-of-date router software.
