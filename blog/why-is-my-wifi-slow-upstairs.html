Why Is My Wi-Fi Slow Upstairs? Simple Fixes

Wi-Fi works well in the room with the router, but upstairs the video freezes and pages load slowly. This is very common, and it is usually the signal, not your internet plan.

Wi-Fi is radio. It gets weaker with distance and with things in the way. Try the steps below in order. You cannot harm anything by trying them.

Step 1: Check where the router sits

  • Is the router in a basement, a closet, a cupboard or behind the TV? Closed spaces and big metal or electronic items weaken the signal.
  • Move it to an open spot, off the floor, ideally near the middle of your home. Keep it in the same place the internet cable reaches, and do not stretch cables.
  • Routers send signal in all directions, so one in a corner of the ground floor sends a lot of it outside.

Step 2: Restart the router

Unplug the router (the box from your internet company) for 30 seconds, then plug it back in. Wait a few minutes for the lights to settle. This clears small glitches.

Step 3: Test the speed in two places

Stand next to the router and run a speed test, then do the same upstairs. Use any well-known speed test website. If it is fast next to the router and slow upstairs, the problem is signal reach. If it is slow in both places, the problem may be your internet plan or the line coming into your home.

Step 4: Know the two Wi-Fi bands

Many routers send two kinds of signal. One reaches farther but is slower. The other is faster but does not travel as far or through floors as well. Phones and laptops pick one automatically, and they sometimes choose badly. If your router shows two names, such as one ending in "5G", try the other name upstairs and see which works better. Do not change anything you are unsure about.

Step 5: Look for things in the way

  • Concrete floors, brick, stone, mirrors and metal heating ducts block Wi-Fi.
  • Fish tanks and large appliances can weaken it.
  • Microwave ovens and some cordless phones can interrupt Wi-Fi while they run.

Step 6: Too many devices, or an old router

Phones, tablets, TVs, cameras and smart speakers all share your Wi-Fi. A router that is many years old may also struggle with a busy home. If yours came from your internet company years ago, they may offer a newer one. Ask them what is available and what it costs.

If it is still slow: extenders and mesh Wi-Fi

  • A Wi-Fi extender repeats the signal. It is cheap and can help one weak spot. It works best placed halfway between the router and the weak area, not in the weak area.
  • A mesh system uses several small units around the home that work as one network. It suits larger or multi-storey homes and usually gives steadier results than a single extender.
  • A cable. For a computer or TV that does not move, a network cable to the router is the most reliable option.

Which one is right depends on your home. We are glad to talk it through, and we will not push you to buy anything.
