Why Is My Wi-Fi Slow Upstairs? Easy Fixes

The Wi-Fi is fine in the kitchen. Upstairs, or down in the basement, videos freeze and pages crawl. This is very common, and you often do not need to buy anything to improve it.

The reason is simple: Wi-Fi is a radio signal, and it gets weaker with distance and with things in the way. Try the steps below in order. You cannot harm anything by trying them.

Step 1: Check if it is really the Wi-Fi

Stand next to the router (the box from your internet company) and do something online, such as playing a video. If it is slow there too, the problem is not the distance. It may be your internet plan or your provider, and moving the router will not help. Restart the router first (Step 2), then call your provider if it is still slow.

If it is fast next to the router and slow upstairs, keep reading. That means the signal is getting weaker on the way.

Step 2: Restart the router

Unplug the router from the power for 30 seconds, then plug it back in. Wait a few minutes. If you have extra Wi-Fi boxes around the house, restart those too.

Step 3: Move the router to a better spot

  • Higher is better. Put it on a shelf, not on the floor.
  • In the open. Not inside a cupboard, behind the TV or in a basement corner.
  • Closer to the middle of the house. If it sits in one corner, half the signal goes outside.
  • Away from other things. Microwaves, cordless phones, fish tanks and large mirrors can all weaken the signal.

The router must stay connected to the wall socket where your internet comes in, so you may only be able to move it a short way. That is fine. Even a small move can help.

Step 4: Try the other Wi-Fi band

Many routers send two kinds of signal, often called 2.4 and 5. The 5 signal is faster but does not travel as far through walls and floors. The 2.4 signal is slower but reaches farther. If your phone or tablet is on the 5 signal upstairs and struggling, switching to the 2.4 one can help. Routers differ in how they show these, so we can look at yours together if the names are not clear.

Step 5: Check the device, not just the router

  • Restart the phone, tablet or computer that is slow.
  • Check whether only one device is slow. If so, the device may need an update or a clean-up.
  • Older devices can have weaker Wi-Fi than newer ones.

Step 6: Think about an extender or mesh system

If the steps above are not enough, you may need more signal in the far rooms. There are two common choices:

  • A Wi-Fi extender is a small plug-in box that repeats the signal. It costs less, but it can slow things down a little and works best placed halfway between the router and the weak room, not in the weak room itself.
  • A mesh system is a set of two or three boxes that work as one network. It costs more, and it is usually the better choice for larger or multi-storey homes.

In some houses, a cable between floors (called Ethernet) or a wired access point works even better. Whether you need any of these depends on your home, so it is worth checking before you spend money.

Why is it slow in the evening only?

If the Wi-Fi is fine in the morning and slow at night in every room, including next to the router, many people in your area are probably online at once, or several devices in your home are streaming. That is a plan or provider question more than a Wi-Fi coverage one.
