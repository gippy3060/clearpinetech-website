Most small businesses in the Lower Mainland don't need a full-time IT person. They need someone who answers when the printer, email or Wi-Fi stops working, and who does the dull maintenance that stops those things breaking in the first place. That is what a managed service provider (MSP) does.

Clearpine Technologies is a managed IT provider based in Abbotsford. We support businesses from Chilliwack to Vancouver on a flat monthly fee per user, under a written agreement that says what is included and what isn't. It suits general offices, trades and construction companies, professional firms, retailers and non-profits, and we have industry pages for clinics, dental offices and law firms where the rules are stricter.

What's included for $95 per user per month

  • Helpdesk. Staff reach us by phone or email during business hours. Most problems are fixed remotely while they wait.
  • Device management and patching. Computers set up to one standard, with updates installed on a schedule instead of whenever someone remembers.
  • Microsoft 365 administration. New starters, leavers, mailboxes, shared calendars, licences and the security settings most small tenants never switch on.
  • Monitoring. Disk, memory, service and backup alerts, so we often see a failing drive or a stalled backup before your staff do.
  • Backup verification. Backups are restored as a test, not only scheduled. We explain why in your backups are probably not working.
  • Asset and licence list. A current list of every computer, account and renewal date, so nothing expires by surprise.

What's billed separately

  • Microsoft 365 licences. We manage them; you pay the licence cost.
  • After-hours support. Evenings and weekends are available and billed separately.
  • On-site visits. Billed separately, but free if we can't fix the problem remotely.
  • Projects. Office moves, new networks, server replacements and large rollouts are quoted up front.

Choosing a provider

How to pick the best MSP in the Lower Mainland for your business

Every IT company calls itself the best. Search results won't tell you which one fits a 12-person office in Langley or a 30-person firm in Burnaby. These questions will, and you can ask them of any provider, including us.

  • Can I see the price before a sales call? A provider that publishes a per-user price is easier to compare. Ask what is included at that price and what is extra.
  • Is there a written agreement with defined response times? “We're very responsive” is not a commitment. Ask to see the agreement before you sign, and check how long it runs and how you leave.
  • How do you prove backups work? The right answer involves restoring files as a test on a schedule. “We get an email every night” is not the same thing.
  • Who comes on site, and how far away are they? Remote support handles most problems, but a failed switch or a new hire's desk setup needs someone in the building. Ask where the technician is based and how on-site time is billed.
  • What security is switched on from day one? At a minimum: multi-factor authentication on email and remote access, patching, endpoint protection and separate admin accounts. See our cybersecurity service for the full list.
  • Do I own the documentation? Passwords, network diagrams and the asset list should be yours, handed over in full if you ever change providers.
  • Will you put the assessment in writing? A written list of findings is something you can take to any provider. A verbal “it all looks a bit old” isn't.

Small business IT support: common questions

How much does small business IT support cost in the Lower Mainland?

Our managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. It covers helpdesk, device management and patching, Microsoft 365 administration, monitoring and backup checks, delivered remotely during business hours. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, and on-site visits are free if the problem cannot be fixed remotely. A 10-person office would pay $950 a month for managed support.

What is the difference between business IT support and an MSP?

An MSP (managed service provider) is a company that looks after your IT for a fixed monthly fee under a written agreement. Business IT support is the broader term, and it can also mean paying a technician by the hour when something breaks. With managed support the provider is paid to keep things working, so patching, monitoring and backup checks happen before problems reach your staff.

How do I choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland?

Look past the word best and check five things: a price you can see before a sales call, a written agreement that states what is included and what is extra, proof that backups are tested by restoring them, a clear answer about who comes on site and how fast, and documentation that you own if you ever leave. A provider that offers a free assessment and gives you the findings in writing makes this comparison much easier.

Is my business too small for managed IT?

Our managed IT plan starts at 5 users. If you have five or more people using computers and email, managed support is usually cheaper than paying for emergencies and lost staff time one problem at a time. Smaller offices can still start with a free assessment.

Do you come on site, or is support only remote?

Most issues are fixed remotely, which is faster for everyone. When something needs hands on site, we come out. On-site visits are billed separately, but they are free when the problem could not be fixed remotely. We are based in Abbotsford and cover the Fraser Valley and Lower Mainland.

Can you work with our existing IT person?

Yes. Co-managed IT is for businesses that already have someone in-house. We add helpdesk overflow, security tooling, project capacity and cover for holidays and sick days, while your IT person stays in charge.

What does the free IT assessment include?

You get a written list of findings ranked by importance. It is free, has no obligation, and can be done on site anywhere within 300 km of Abbotsford, or remotely.
