What small business IT support includes

Everything below is part of the monthly price and delivered remotely during business hours:

  • Helpdesk for your staff — reachable by phone and email, with response and resolution targets written into the agreement and reported against monthly.
  • Computer and device management — standard setups for every workstation, updates and patches on a schedule, and new laptops ready to use on day one.
  • Microsoft 365 administration — adding and removing users, licences, mailboxes, shared calendars, Teams and security settings. More on Microsoft 365 support.
  • Backups that are tested — we restore files on purpose to prove the backups work, rather than finding out during an emergency. (Why this matters: your backups are probably not working.)
  • Proactive monitoring — alerts for full disks, failing services and missed backups, so problems are caught before staff notice them.
  • Security basics done properly — multi-factor authentication, managed antivirus, sensible admin accounts and offboarding that really removes access. See cybersecurity services for firewalls, segmentation and assessments.
  • Asset and licence inventory — a current list of what you own, when warranties run out and what renews when, so nothing ambushes the budget.
  • Plain-English advice — a senior person to ask before you buy a new phone system, sign a software contract or open a second location.

Managed IT vs break-fix: which suits a small business?

Break-fix means calling a technician when something breaks and paying by the hour. It is simple and can be cheaper for a very small office with few problems. The catch is that nobody is paid to prevent problems, so patching, backups and security tend to get done only when something has already gone wrong.

Managed IT support is a fixed monthly fee. Because the provider absorbs the cost of most problems, it has every reason to prevent them: patching on a schedule, monitoring, testing backups and tightening security. You also get a predictable budget and someone who already knows your setup when you call.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search for “best MSP in the Lower Mainland” and you’ll get directory rankings and sponsored lists. The best provider is the one that fits your business, and you can test that with a few questions. Here are the ones we’d ask, with our own answers:

  1. What exactly does the monthly price include and exclude? Ask for it in writing. Ours: helpdesk, device management, Microsoft 365 admin, monitoring, backup testing and security basics are included; on-site visits, after-hours work and licences are not, and on-site visits are free if a problem can’t be fixed remotely.
  2. Are response times in the contract? A response target that only appears in a sales deck isn’t one. Ours are written into the service agreement and reported monthly.
  3. How are backups tested? “We back up nightly” is not the same as “we restored a file last month to prove it works”. We test by restoring.
  4. Who will I actually talk to? At a small MSP you get a senior person. At a larger one, ask how tickets move between tiers and who covers when your main contact is away.
  5. Can they reach my office? Remote support handles most problems, but some need hands on site. We’re based in Abbotsford and cover the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver.
  6. What happens if I leave? You should get your documentation, network diagrams and admin passwords without a fight. We hand documentation over as a matter of course.
  7. What’s the contract term? Ours is 1 year. Ask any provider how renewals and cancellations work before you sign.

Talk to two or three providers, compare written answers, and ask for references from businesses like yours.
