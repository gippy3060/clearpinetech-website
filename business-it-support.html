What small business IT support includes

Our managed IT support covers the day-to-day work that keeps a small office running:

  • Helpdesk — your staff call or email a real person when something breaks, with response and resolution targets written into the agreement.
  • Computers and devices — standard setups for new staff, patching on a schedule, and clean offboarding when someone leaves.
  • Microsoft 365 — users, mailboxes, licences, shared files and the security settings most small tenants never had turned on.
  • Backups that are tested — not just scheduled. A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a plan. (Here’s why your backups probably aren’t working.)
  • Monitoring — disk, backup and service alerts so failures show up on our screen before your staff notice.
  • Cybersecurity basics done properly — multi-factor authentication, firewall configuration, network segmentation for guest Wi-Fi and card terminals, and email protection. See our cybersecurity services.
  • An asset and licence list — kept current, so renewals and old hardware don’t surprise your budget.

When a business already has an IT person, we can work alongside them instead. That’s co-managed IT: after-hours cover, a second set of hands for projects, and someone to call during holidays and sick days.

Managed IT, break-fix or an in-house hire?

There are three common ways a small business gets IT help, and each suits a different stage:

  • Break-fix (hourly) — you call someone when something breaks and pay by the hour. Cheap in a quiet month, but nobody is watching backups, patches or security in between, and the worst problems tend to arrive all at once.
  • Managed IT (monthly) — a fixed monthly price per user covers support plus the preventive work. The provider is paid to keep things from breaking, so incentives line up with yours.
  • An in-house IT person — makes sense once you have enough daily work to keep someone busy, but one person can’t cover every skill, every holiday and every sick day.

We’ve written up both comparisons in detail: managed IT vs in-house IT for small businesses and hiring an independent IT contractor vs an MSP.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search “best MSP” in BC and half the results are about the Medical Services Plan. Narrow it to IT and you’ll find dozens of managed service providers between Vancouver and Chilliwack, all saying they’re responsive and proactive. The best one for you is the one whose answers hold up in writing. Ask every provider, including us, the same questions:

  1. What exactly does the monthly price include? Helpdesk, patching, backups, monitoring, security tools, Microsoft 365 administration. And what is billed on top: after-hours, on-site visits, projects, licences.
  2. What are the response times, and are they in the contract? A target that only lives on a website isn’t a commitment.
  3. Who will we actually talk to? A named person who knows your setup, or a ticket queue?
  4. How do you test backups? Ask when they last did a test restore for a client, and what they restored.
  5. Where are your technicians, and how fast can someone be on site? A provider an hour away will either be slow or build the travel into the price.
  6. How long is the agreement, and how do we leave? Ask what happens at the end of the term.
  7. Will you hand over every password and document if we part ways? If the answer is hesitant, that’s your answer.
  8. Do you understand our kind of business? A dental office, a law firm and a trucking dispatcher have very different problems.

Our answers: the price and what’s included are on this page, the agreement is 1 year with a written scope, and every engagement ends with a handover of documentation, credentials and knowledge, not a lock-in. We also choose mainstream, well-supported tools that another firm could pick up, which is one of the things we believe about how IT should be run.

What is a managed service provider (MSP)?

In IT, an MSP is a company that looks after your computers, network, email and security for a fixed monthly fee, instead of billing by the hour each time something breaks. (In BC, “MSP” is also the Medical Services Plan, which is unrelated.)
