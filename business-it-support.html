Managed IT for offices of 5 to 50 people · Fraser Valley & Metro Vancouver

Most small businesses don’t need an IT department. They need someone who answers when the email stops working, keeps the computers patched, checks that the backups actually restore, and tells them straight what is worth spending money on.

That is what Clearpine does for small offices across the Lower Mainland. We are an Abbotsford-based managed service provider (MSP) supporting businesses from Chilliwack to Vancouver, with one clear monthly price and local people who can come on site.

What business IT support includes

  • Helpdesk — staff can reach us by phone or email for everyday problems: logins, printers, email, slow computers, software that won’t open.
  • Microsoft 365 administration — new and departing staff, licences, mailboxes, shared calendars, Teams and SharePoint, and security settings.
  • Endpoint management and patching — laptops and desktops set up the same way and updated on a schedule, not when someone remembers.
  • Backups that are tested — scheduled backups are only half the job. We test restores, because a failed backup is usually discovered during the emergency. (Background: Your backups are probably not working.)
  • Security basics done properly — multi-factor authentication, firewall configuration, email filtering and network segmentation scoped to a small business, not an enterprise. See cybersecurity.
  • Proactive monitoring — alerts on disks, services and backups so problems surface before staff notice.
  • Asset and licence records — so renewals and end-of-life hardware don’t surprise your budget.
  • On-site help when it’s needed — network, Wi-Fi, printer and hardware work in person across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search “best MSP in the Lower Mainland” and you will find plenty of providers calling themselves the best. A better test is how they answer these questions:

  1. What does it cost, and what is included? Get a per-user or flat price in writing, and a list of what is extra (on-site, after-hours, projects, licences).
  2. What are the response targets? They should be written into the agreement, not promised on the sales call.
  3. How are backups tested? Ask when they last did a test restore for a client your size.
  4. Who comes on site, and from where? “Local” should mean someone who can reach your office the same day, not a remote-only team in another province.
  5. What are the contract term and exit terms? Know how long you are committed and what happens to your data and passwords if you leave.
  6. Do you keep admin access and documentation? Your business should own its Microsoft 365 tenant, domain and passwords, with documentation you can hand to someone else.
  7. Do they understand your industry? A dental office, a law firm and a warehouse have very different IT problems.

For more on the decision, read managed IT vs. hiring in-house and an independent IT contractor vs. an MSP.
