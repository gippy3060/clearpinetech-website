Small Business IT Support in the Lower Mainland

Managed IT for small and growing offices · Fraser Valley & Metro Vancouver

Most small businesses in the Lower Mainland don’t need an IT department. They need one dependable team that answers when something breaks, keeps Microsoft 365 and the computers patched and secure, and can prove the backups actually restore. That is what Clearpine’s business IT support does, from our base in Abbotsford for businesses across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver.

What business IT support includes

Our managed IT support runs under a written service agreement with defined response times. Every plan includes:

  • Helpdesk: staff reach us by phone and email, with response and resolution targets reported monthly.
  • Computer and device management: standard builds, configuration and patching on a schedule, not when someone remembers.
  • Microsoft 365 administration: new and departing staff, licences, mailboxes, multi-factor authentication and security settings.
  • Backup verification: backups are test-restored, not just scheduled. (Here’s why that matters.)
  • Proactive monitoring: disk, memory, service and backup alerts, so we see failures before your staff do.
  • Asset and licence inventory: so renewals and ageing hardware don’t surprise the budget.

Beyond day-to-day support we also handle cybersecurity (firewalls, MFA, network segmentation), hardware and network deployment for new offices and moves, and co-managed IT for businesses that already have an IT person and want backup.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search for the “best MSP in the Lower Mainland” and most of what comes back is written by the providers themselves. There is no independent ranking that settles it, so the useful question is which provider is best for your business. Ask every provider you talk to, including us, these questions:

  1. Are response times in the contract? “We’re very responsive” is not a service level. Ask for written response and resolution targets and how they report against them.
  2. Is the price per user and what does it exclude? Get the monthly number, then ask exactly what is billed on top: licences, on-site visits, after-hours work, projects.
  3. How do you know my backups work? The right answer involves scheduled test restores, not a green tick on a dashboard.
  4. Who will actually answer the phone? Ask where the helpdesk is and whether you’ll deal with the same small group of people.
  5. How fast can someone be on site? Most issues are fixed remotely, but for a failed network switch or a new office, distance matters. Ask where they drive from.
  6. What happens if we leave? Contract length, notice period, and whether you get your passwords, documentation and admin access back without a fight.
  7. Have you worked with businesses like ours? A dental office, a law firm and a contractor’s office have very different software and privacy obligations.
  8. How do you handle privacy and cyber insurance? Ask how they help with BC privacy obligations (PIPA) and the security questions on cyber-insurance applications.

Our answers: written response times, $95/user/month with exclusions listed above, tested backups, a local team based in Abbotsford, and a free assessment so you can judge for yourself before signing anything.

Managed IT vs. the alternatives

A managed service provider isn’t the only way to support a small office. The honest trade-offs:

  • Break-fix (pay per call): cheap in a quiet month, expensive in a bad one, and nobody is watching patches or backups in between.
  • An independent contractor: personal and often affordable, but you depend on one person’s availability. See independent IT contractor vs. an MSP.
  • Hiring in-house: makes sense once you’re large enough to keep someone busy full time; below that, salary plus tools usually costs more. See managed IT vs. hiring in-house.
  • Co-managed IT: keep your in-house person and add an MSP for coverage, security and projects.
