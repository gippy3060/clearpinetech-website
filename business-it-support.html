Buyer's guide

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search "best MSP in the Lower Mainland" and you'll find dozens of firms that all say they're the best. The right provider for you depends on your size, your industry and how you like to work. These are the questions we'd ask if we were hiring an IT company ourselves, and you should ask them of us too.

  1. What exactly is in the monthly price? Ask for a written list. "Unlimited support" can still exclude on-site visits, after-hours calls, new-user setups or projects.
  2. What are the response times, and are they in the contract? A target in a sales deck isn't the same as one in the agreement.
  3. Who will actually do the work? Will you deal with the same few people, or a different technician every call?
  4. How quickly can someone get to our office? Remote support fixes most problems, but a failed firewall or a new office still needs a person on site. Distance from their office matters.
  5. How do they handle security? Ask whether multi-factor authentication, patching and endpoint protection are standard for every client, or optional extras.
  6. When did they last test a restore of a client's backup? The answer tells you whether backups are checked or just assumed.
  7. Do they know your industry? Clinics, law firms and other regulated offices have privacy obligations and specialist software that a general provider may not know.
  8. What happens if you leave? You should own your accounts, passwords and documentation, and get them handed over without a fight.
  9. How long is the contract, and what does it cost to get out? Read the term, the notice period and any onboarding or exit fees.
  10. Can you talk to a current client? Ask for a reference from a business similar to yours.

Options

Managed IT, break-fix or co-managed?

Break-fix (pay per call)

You call an IT company when something breaks and pay for the time. It can work for a very small office with simple needs, but nobody is watching your backups, patches or security in between, and every emergency is billed at the time it happens.

Managed IT (monthly per user)

A fixed monthly fee covers support and the routine maintenance that prevents problems. Costs are predictable, and the provider has a reason to stop problems happening rather than profit from them. This is what most small businesses with five or more staff choose. See what's included in our managed IT support.

Co-managed IT

If you already have an IT person or a small internal team, we can work alongside them: covering the helpdesk, security tools, projects or holidays, while they keep ownership. See co-managed IT.

Questions

Small business IT support: common questions

Who is the best MSP in the Lower Mainland?

There isn't one answer: the best managed service provider for a 6-person accounting office in Langley isn't the same as for a 60-person firm in Vancouver. Look for clear pricing, response times written into the contract, real security standards, experience with your type of business, and an easy exit. The questions above will help you compare providers, including us.

How much does IT support cost for a small business in BC?

Managed IT usually costs a fixed amount per user per month. Ours is $95 per user per month (minimum 5 users, 1-year agreement), with Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits billed separately. On-site visits are free if we can't fix the problem remotely. For fewer than 5 users, we can set up custom billing.

Do you offer on-site IT support?

Yes. Most problems are fixed remotely, which is faster for you. When something needs a person at your office, we come on site. On-site visits are billed separately, but they're free if we couldn't fix the problem remotely.

We only have a few staff. Is managed IT worth it?

Often, yes, if your business depends on email, files and a few key applications. Our standard minimum is 5 users, but we offer custom billing for smaller offices. A free IT assessment will show whether it makes sense for you.

Can we switch to you from our current IT provider?

Yes. Check your current contract's notice period first. We then work with you to collect admin access, passwords and documentation and take over support. If you'd rather keep your current provider, the free assessment can still be a useful second opinion.
