What small business IT support includes

Our managed IT support runs under a written service agreement with defined response times, so expectations are written down rather than assumed. Every plan includes:

  • Helpdesk — reachable by phone and email, with response and resolution targets reported monthly.
  • Endpoint management — standard builds for workstations and laptops, and patching on a schedule rather than when someone remembers.
  • Microsoft 365 administration — new staff, departures, licences, mailboxes, shared calendars and security settings such as multi-factor authentication.
  • Backup verification — backups tested, not just scheduled. Most failed recoveries are discovered during the recovery.
  • Proactive monitoring — disk, memory, service and backup alerts, so failures surface before your staff notice them.
  • Asset and licence inventory — kept current, so renewals and end-of-life hardware don’t ambush your budget.

Beyond the monthly plan we also handle cybersecurity work such as firewalls, MFA and network segmentation, hardware deployment for new offices and refreshes, IT projects and office moves, and co-managed IT if you already have an IT person who needs backup.

What it costs

Managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users on a 1-year agreement. Fewer than 5 users? We can set up custom billing.

  • Included: everything in the list above, delivered remotely during business hours.
  • On-site visits: billed separately, but free if we can’t fix the problem remotely.
  • After-hours support: billed separately.

For context, published per-user prices from Lower Mainland providers that we’ve seen range from about $80 to well over $150 per user per month, and the bigger difference is usually what the price covers. A low headline number that excludes security tools, Microsoft 365 work or on-site time can end up costing more. Whoever you talk to, ask for a written list of what’s included and what’s billed extra. Signs your business has outgrown ad-hoc IT

  • The same problems come back every few weeks and nobody has time to find the cause.
  • One staff member (often the owner) loses hours every month fixing computers instead of doing their job.
  • Nobody can say for certain when a backup was last restored, not just run.
  • Former employees may still have access to email, files or shared passwords.
  • Your cyber-insurance renewal asks about MFA, backups or patching and you’re not sure how to answer.
  • Laptops, the firewall or the server are past their supported life and nobody is tracking it.
  • You’re paying an hourly IT contractor and the invoices are unpredictable.

If three or more of those sound familiar, a monthly managed plan usually costs less in lost time than it costs on the invoice. Our managed IT vs in-house IT comparison and independent contractor vs MSP guide go into the trade-offs.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

There are dozens of managed service providers (MSPs) between Vancouver and Chilliwack, and every one of them will tell you they’re the best. Rankings and review sites are a starting point, but many listings have only a handful of reviews. A better test is how a provider answers these questions, and we’re happy to be held to the same list:

  1. What exactly does the monthly price include, and what is billed extra? Get it in writing: helpdesk, security tools, Microsoft 365 work, on-site visits, after-hours, projects.
  2. Are response times written into the agreement? “We’re very responsive” is not a service level.
  3. Who answers when you call, and where are they? Ask whether support is local, elsewhere in Canada or offshore, and whether a technician can be on site when it matters.
  4. How are backups tested? Ask when they last did a test restore for a client, and how they’d prove it to you.
  5. What security is included by default? MFA, patching, endpoint protection and email filtering should be standard, not an upsell.
  6. How long is the contract, and what happens when you leave? You should get your passwords, documentation and admin access back without a fight.
  7. Who owns the documentation? You should be able to see a current list of your devices, accounts and licences at any time.
  8. Do they know your kind of business? A dental office, a law firm and a contractor’s office have very different software and privacy obligations.
  9. Can they give references from businesses your size? A firm built for 200-seat offices may not be the right fit for an office of eight.
  10. Will they assess before they sell? A good provider should be able to tell you what’s actually wrong before quoting a fix.

Get two or three written quotes and compare them line by line. Our free IT assessment is designed to be one of those quotes: we review what you have, tell you plainly what matters, and you decide.

Managed IT, break-fix or in-house?

  • Break-fix — you call when something breaks and pay by the hour. Cheap in a quiet month, but nobody is patching, monitoring or testing backups in between, so the problems that do happen tend to be bigger.
  • Managed IT — a fixed monthly fee for the helpdesk plus the preventive work. Predictable cost, and the provider has a reason to prevent problems rather than bill for them.
  • In-house IT — a dedicated employee. Makes sense once you’re large enough to keep one person busy, but one person can’t cover vacations, sick days and every specialty. That is where co-managed IT fits.
