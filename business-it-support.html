Most small businesses in the Lower Mainland do not need a full-time IT employee. They need someone reliable to call when a laptop will not start or email stops syncing, and someone careful enough to stop the bigger problems (a ransomware infection, a lost laptop full of customer data, a backup that turns out to be empty) before they happen.

What our business IT support includes

  • A helpdesk that answers. Reach us by phone or email. Response and resolution targets are written into the service agreement and reported on monthly, so expectations are in the contract, not left to hope.
  • Computers that are looked after. Standard setups for every workstation and laptop, updates installed on a schedule, endpoint protection that is watched, and the ability to lock or wipe a lost device.
  • Microsoft 365 administration. New staff set up on day one, leavers removed on their last day, licences kept tidy, mailboxes managed and security settings switched on. You pay Microsoft for the licences; we run them.
  • Cybersecurity basics done properly. Multi-factor authentication on email and remote access, a firewall that is configured rather than left on factory defaults, a separate guest Wi-Fi, and accounts with only the access people need. More on our cybersecurity services.
  • Backups that are tested. Scheduling a backup is easy; restoring from it is the part that fails. We test restores. Read why in your backups are probably not working.
  • Monitoring. Alerts for full disks, failing drives, stopped services and missed backups, so problems surface before your staff notice them.
  • An asset and licence list. What you own, how old it is and when it renews, so replacing hardware becomes a planned budget line instead of a surprise.
  • Documentation you keep. Network diagram, passwords held securely, and the settings we applied. If you ever leave us, another provider can pick it up.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search for "best MSP" and every provider will say it is them. We would rather give you the questions to ask. Any good managed service provider, including us, should answer these clearly and in writing:

  1. Who answers when I call? A named team or a call centre? Will the same people know my setup next month?
  2. What response times are in the contract? "We are very responsive" is not a commitment. Ask for the numbers and how they are reported.
  3. What is included in the monthly price, and what is extra? On-site visits, after-hours work, projects, new computer setup and security tools are common add-ons.
  4. How are backups tested? Ask when they last performed a test restore for a client and what it found.
  5. Is multi-factor authentication switched on for every account? It is one of the most effective security controls and should be standard, not an upsell.
  6. How long is the contract and how do I leave? Check the notice period and what happens at renewal.
  7. Do I get my passwords and documentation if we part ways? The answer should be yes, without a fee.
  8. Can they come on site? A provider based hours away may be remote-only in practice. Ask how quickly someone can be at your door.
