Most small businesses in the Lower Mainland don't need an IT department. They need someone who picks up when the printer, the email or the line-of-business software stops, and who keeps the quiet work done so those calls happen less often. That's what our managed IT support covers:

  • Helpdesk for your staff. Phone and email support with response and resolution targets written into the agreement and reported monthly.
  • Computers and devices. Standard setups for new staff, scheduled patching, disk encryption and a clean offboarding when someone leaves.
  • Microsoft 365 administration. Users, licences, mailboxes, shared files and the security settings that stop most account takeovers. You pay Microsoft for the licences; we manage them.
  • Security basics done properly. Multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection, admin accounts kept separate, and phishing defences. More in our cybersecurity services.
  • Backups that are tested. Backups checked and restores tried, because most failed recoveries are only discovered during the recovery. See why backups quietly fail.
  • Monitoring. Disk, service and backup alerts so problems surface before your staff notice them.
  • Network and Wi-Fi. Firewall, router and Wi-Fi kept current, with guest Wi-Fi kept away from business computers.
  • Vendors and records. An up-to-date list of your devices, licences and renewals, and one number to call instead of five vendors.

Buyer's guide

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search "best MSP Lower Mainland" and you'll find dozens of managed service providers, from downtown Vancouver firms built for 200-person offices to one-person shops in the Fraser Valley. There's no single best one. There's the one that fits your size, your industry and your location. These are the questions that separate them, whoever you end up hiring:

  1. What does it cost, in writing, and what's not included? Ask for the per-user or per-device price and a list of everything billed on top: on-site visits, after-hours calls, projects, new-computer setup, licences.
  2. Are response times in the contract? "We're very responsive" isn't a service level. Look for response and resolution targets you can hold them to, and a monthly report against them.
  3. Who comes on site, and from where? A provider an hour and a half away will struggle with same-day visits. Ask where the technicians are based and how visits are charged.
  4. How long is the contract, and how do you leave? Know the term, the notice period and what they hand back at the end.
  5. Who owns the admin passwords and documentation? You should. A good provider keeps documentation you can take to someone else if you ever switch.
  6. How often are backups restore-tested? "We back up nightly" isn't an answer. Ask when they last restored a file or a full system for a client, and how it went.
  7. What security is standard, not an upsell? Multi-factor authentication, patching and endpoint protection should be part of the base service.
  8. Do they work with businesses like yours? Ask for references from offices your size and, if you're regulated, in your industry: clinics, dental offices or law firms.

We publish our answers on this page and on our services page. If you're comparing providers, our free IT assessment is a no-obligation way to see how we work, and the written findings are yours to keep whoever you choose.

Small business IT support: common questions

How much does small business IT support cost in the Lower Mainland?

Our managed IT support is $95 per user per month, minimum 5 users on a 1-year agreement, with custom billing for fewer than 5 users. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, and on-site visits are free if we can't fix the problem remotely. Published prices from other Lower Mainland providers we reviewed in late 2026 ranged from roughly $70 to over $200 per user per month, so always compare what's included, not just the headline number.

How do I choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland for my business?

There's no single best MSP; there's the best fit for your size, industry and location. Ask for the price in writing and what's excluded, response times in the contract, who comes on site and how far they travel, contract length and exit terms, who holds your admin passwords and documentation, how often backups are restore-tested, and for references from businesses your size. See the full checklist.

Is managed IT worth it for a business with 5 to 20 staff?

Usually, once the cost of downtime and the time staff spend fixing their own IT add up. A full-time IT hire costs far more than per-user support for a small team, and one person can't cover holidays or sick days. Very small or very stable offices can do fine with occasional break-fix help; the free IT assessment will tell you honestly which fits.

Do you provide on-site IT support or only remote?

Both. Most issues are fixed remotely, which is fastest. When a visit is needed we come on site from Abbotsford across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver. On-site visits are billed separately, but they're free if we can't fix the problem remotely.

Can you take over from our current IT provider?

Yes. We start with a free assessment, then plan the handover: admin accounts, licences, backups, documentation and vendor contacts move across in an agreed order so nothing is left orphaned. If you have an in-house IT person, co-managed IT lets you keep them and add support.

Do you support Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace?

Yes. Microsoft 365 administration, including users, licences, mailboxes and security settings, is part of managed IT support; you pay the licence cost directly. We also review Google Workspace setups as part of the free IT assessment.
