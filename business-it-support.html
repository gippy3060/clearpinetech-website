Most small businesses don’t need a full-time IT department. They need someone who answers when the printer, the email or the shared drive stops working, and who quietly does the patching, backups and security work that keeps those calls rare. That is what business IT support from a managed service provider (MSP) is meant to be.

Clearpine Technologies is an MSP based in Abbotsford. We support small and mid-sized businesses across the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver: professional offices, clinics, law firms, trades and service companies, non-profits and growing teams that have outgrown “the person in the office who’s good with computers.”

What’s included in our business IT support

  • Helpdesk for your staff — reachable by phone and email, with response and resolution targets set out in a written service agreement.
  • Computer management and patching — standard setups for every workstation and laptop, with Windows and application updates applied on a schedule rather than when someone remembers.
  • Microsoft 365 administration — new staff, departures, licences, mailboxes, shared folders and security settings. We manage the licences; you pay Microsoft’s licence cost.
  • Security basics done properly — multi-factor authentication, managed endpoint protection, firewall configuration and accounts that are actually removed when someone leaves. See cybersecurity services.
  • Backups that are tested — not just scheduled. We check that files can actually be restored. (Here is why untested backups quietly fail.)
  • Monitoring — disk, memory, service and backup alerts, so failing hardware is replaced before it takes a day of work with it.
  • Asset and licence inventory — a current list of your devices, warranties and renewals, so ageing hardware and expiring licences don’t ambush the budget.

Many Lower Mainland providers don’t publish a price at all; we’d rather you know the number before the first call. Projects such as an office move, a server replacement or a new-site build are quoted separately.

Managed IT, break-fix or in-house?

There are three common ways a small business handles IT, and each fits a different situation:

  • Break-fix (pay per call) — cheap in a quiet month, but nobody is paid to prevent problems, so patching, backups and security tend to slip until something breaks.
  • In-house IT hire — makes sense once you have enough work to keep a person busy, but one person can’t cover every skill, every vacation or every 2 a.m. problem. Compare the numbers in managed IT vs in-house IT for small businesses.
  • Managed IT (MSP) — a predictable monthly cost per user, with prevention built in. If you already have an IT person, co-managed IT adds backup without replacing them.

How to choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland

Search for “best MSP Lower Mainland” and you’ll mostly find providers describing themselves. Directory rankings rarely explain their method. A better test is to put the same questions to two or three providers and compare the answers side by side:

  1. What is the price per user, and what is excluded? Ask specifically about on-site visits, after-hours work, licences and projects.
  2. What are the response times, in writing? A promise on a website is not a service agreement.
  3. Who comes on site, and how far do they travel? A Vancouver-only firm may charge travel time to Langley or Chilliwack.
  4. Who owns the admin passwords and documentation? If you leave, you should get everything back without a fight.
  5. How are backups tested, and how often? “We have backups” isn’t the same as “we restored a file last month.”
  6. How long is the contract, and how do you leave? Know the term and the exit process before you sign.
  7. Have they supported businesses like yours? A dental office, a law firm and a construction company each have different software and privacy obligations.

Our answers: $95 per user per month with the exclusions listed above, a 1-year agreement, on-site visits free when remote fixes fail, an Abbotsford base covering the whole Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver, and documentation that belongs to you. Business IT support FAQ

How much does IT support cost for a small business in the Lower Mainland?

Clearpine’s managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. That covers helpdesk, device management, patching, Microsoft 365 administration, backup checks and monitoring, delivered remotely during business hours. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, and on-site visits are free if we can’t fix the problem remotely. A 10-person office works out to $950 a month before licences.

Do you come on site, or is it all remote?

Most issues are fixed remotely, which is faster for you. When a problem needs hands on site and can’t be fixed remotely, the visit is free. We’re based in Abbotsford and cover the Fraser Valley and Metro Vancouver, and free IT assessments can be done on site anywhere within 300 km of Abbotsford.

Which areas do you cover?

Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Mission, Langley, Surrey, Maple Ridge, New Westminster, Burnaby, Vancouver, North Vancouver and the Sea-to-Sky corridor up to Whistler, plus remote support anywhere in Canada.

We already have an IT person. Can you still help?

Yes. Co-managed IT keeps your in-house person and adds after-hours and overflow coverage, security tooling and help with projects. We agree in writing who owns what.

How do we switch from our current IT provider?

Start with a free IT assessment so you know what you have. We then collect admin access, documentation and licence details from your current provider, take over monitoring and backups first, and move the helpdesk last so nobody is left without support during the change.

How do we choose the best MSP in the Lower Mainland for our business?

Ask for a published per-user price and what it excludes, written response times, how on-site visits are billed, who holds your admin passwords and documentation if you leave, how backups are tested, and whether they have worked with businesses like yours. Then compare two or three providers on the same list.
