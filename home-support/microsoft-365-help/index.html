Before you call

Three things that make a Microsoft 365 session quicker

Don't worry if you can't find all of them. We can work it out together.

  1. The email address you use for Microsoft. This is your Microsoft account. It is often an Outlook.com, Hotmail or Live address, but it can also be a Gmail or other address. Please don't tell us your password; you type it yourself.
  2. Your phone, nearby. Microsoft may send a code by text or ask you to approve the sign-in in an app. Keep the phone next to you during the session.
  3. How you got Office. A yearly or monthly subscription (Microsoft 365 Personal or Family), a one-time purchase (such as Office Home), or through work or school. If you have a printed card or a receipt email, keep it handy.

If your Microsoft 365 comes from your job or school, their IT staff control it, and some settings can only be changed by them. Running a small business? See our business IT services.

Good to know

Microsoft 365 in plain words

Subscription or one-time purchase. Microsoft 365 Personal and Family are paid every month or year and keep updating. Office Home is paid once and is usually for one computer. Both are linked to your Microsoft account.

Your Microsoft account matters most. Most problems come down to which account is signed in. You can check it in Word under File, then Account.

Only use Microsoft's own websites. Microsoft's pages end in microsoft.com or office.com. Websites with other names that offer to "activate Office" or sell cheap product keys are not Microsoft. Please type the address yourself rather than using a search ad.

There is a free option. Microsoft offers free web versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint that work in a web browser. For simple letters they may be enough, and we will tell you honestly if they are.

What remote support cannot do

  • Sell you a Microsoft 365 subscription or product key
  • Get you back into a Microsoft account without your password or recovery details (we can help you fill in Microsoft's own recovery form)
  • Ask Microsoft for a refund on your behalf
  • Change settings on a work or school account that your employer or school controls
  • Install pirated or "cracked" copies of Office

Questions

Microsoft 365 help FAQ

Word or Excel says "Unlicensed Product". What does that mean?

It means the app cannot confirm that you have a valid Microsoft 365 subscription or Office purchase. The usual reasons are an expired subscription, being signed in with a different Microsoft account from the one that paid, or two copies of Office on the same computer. Open Word, click File, then Account, and check which account is shown. Our article on the Unlicensed Product message has step-by-step fixes.

Can I put Microsoft 365 on my new computer?

Usually yes. With a Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscription, you sign in to your Microsoft account on the new computer and install the apps from there. A one-time Office purchase is also tied to your Microsoft account, but it may be limited to one computer. We can check what you have and install it with you.

How do I share Microsoft 365 Family with my family?

The person who pays can invite up to five other people from their Microsoft account, under Services and subscriptions, then Sharing. Each person uses their own Microsoft account and gets their own storage. We can send the invitations with you and help each person accept.

My OneDrive says it is full. Will I lose my files?

Not straight away, but new files stop being saved to OneDrive, and on a free account you may stop being able to send or receive Outlook.com email. Do not start deleting files in a hurry: in a synced folder, deleting a file on the computer also deletes it from OneDrive. Our OneDrive storage article explains safe steps.

Is the free version of Word any good?

For letters and simple documents, often yes. Microsoft offers free web versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint that you use in a web browser after signing in with a free Microsoft account. They have fewer features than the paid apps. We can help you decide whether you need to pay at all.

I got an email saying my Microsoft 365 renewed for a large amount. Should I call the number?

No. Do not call numbers in the email or click its links. Fake renewal emails are a common scam. Type account.microsoft.com into your browser yourself and look under Services and subscriptions and Order history to see what is real.
