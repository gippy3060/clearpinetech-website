What to expect

Help that is calm and unhurried

1. You call us

Tell us what is wrong in your own words. You do not need to know any tech terms. We will ask a few simple questions.

If we need to look at your screen, we explain exactly what will happen first. Nothing starts until you say yes.

Stay safe

How to spot a tech-support scam

Scammers target older adults. Here are the warning signs, in plain words.

  1. They contact you first. A real company will not phone, text or email you out of the blue to say your computer has a problem. Neither will we.
  2. A loud pop-up says your computer is infected. Real warnings do not show a phone number to call or lock your screen. Close the window, or switch the computer off if it will not close.
  3. They want to be paid in gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency. No honest business asks for these. Real companies take a normal credit or debit card.
  4. They say it is urgent and tell you to keep it secret. Pressure and secrecy are tricks. Take your time and talk to a family member.
  5. They ask you to let them into your computer right away, or to log in to your bank. Never open your online banking while someone else can see your screen.
  6. They say they are from your bank, the government, Microsoft or Apple. Hang up and call the number on your card, bill or the company's real website.

If you think you have been scammed: stop talking to them, turn off your internet or switch off the computer, and call your bank at once if money or card details were shared. Then report it: in Canada, to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; in the USA, at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. We can also check whether remote-access software was left on your computer.
