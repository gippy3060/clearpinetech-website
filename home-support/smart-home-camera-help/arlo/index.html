Plain steps, no rush. You can ask us to repeat anything. This covers Arlo outdoor and indoor cameras, Arlo video doorbells, base stations and SmartHubs, and the Arlo app on iPhone and Android phones.

Camera offline

Usually a flat battery, a base station that lost its connection, or a weak Wi-Fi signal. We check each one with you and get it back online.

Try it yourself first

Arlo camera offline: what to check

Menus can differ a little between Arlo models and app versions. If these steps do not help, call us.

  1. Check the battery. Open the Arlo app and look at the battery level for the camera. If it is low, charge it fully. Battery cameras go offline when they run flat.
  2. Find out how it connects. Look near your router for a small Arlo box joined to it by a cable. That is a base station or SmartHub. If you have one, your cameras talk to it, not straight to your Wi-Fi. If you do not, your cameras join your Wi-Fi directly.
  3. If you have a base station or SmartHub: check that it has power and that its cable is pushed firmly into the router. Unplug its power for 30 seconds, plug it back in, and wait a few minutes for its lights to settle.
  4. Restart the router. Unplug your router (and modem, if you have a separate one) for 30 seconds, plug it back in, and wait about five minutes.
  5. Did anything change? A new router, a new internet provider, or a new Wi-Fi name or password? Move the base station's cable to the new router. Cameras that join Wi-Fi directly have to be joined to the new network in the Arlo app.
  6. Look at the signal. The Arlo app shows signal bars for each camera in its settings. One or two bars means the camera is too far from the router or hub. Moving the hub, or adding a Wi-Fi extender or mesh system, can help.
  7. Know when to stop. If a camera shows no light at all after a full charge, or a wired doorbell has no power, it may be the device itself or the wiring. That needs an electrician or Arlo's own support, and we will tell you so.

Cold winters: in freezing weather, Arlo batteries drain faster and may not charge outside at all. Bring the battery (or the camera) inside to charge at room temperature. If you use a solar panel, it gives much less power on short, grey winter days.

Wi-Fi doesn't reach the camera? See Wi-Fi extender and mesh setup or Wi-Fi and router help. Have a Ring doorbell as well? See independent help with your Ring doorbell or camera. Other cameras and smart speakers are on our smart home and camera help page.

Good to know

Things that are specific to Arlo

Base station, SmartHub or straight to Wi-Fi. Older Arlo cameras need a base station or SmartHub. Many newer cameras can join your Wi-Fi directly, and some can do either. Knowing which you have tells us where to look first, so we start there together.

Many Arlo cameras use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Many routers send out two kinds of Wi-Fi, often called 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. Many Arlo cameras that join Wi-Fi directly need the 2.4 GHz one. If your router uses separate names for each, the camera needs the 2.4 GHz name. We check this with you.

Recordings and plans. Without a paid plan, most Arlo cameras still send alerts and show live video. Saving recordings to Arlo's cloud needs a paid plan, and some base stations and SmartHubs can save to a USB drive or memory card instead. Arlo sets the plans and prices, so check them in the Arlo app. We do not sell Arlo plans.

Arlo and Netgear are not the same thing any more. Arlo started as part of Netgear and is now a separate company. If your Netgear router is the problem, see independent help with your Netgear router.

Two-step verification. Arlo checks it is really you when you sign in on a new phone, with a code or a notification. That code is only for you. Never read it to someone who calls or messages you, even if they say they are from Arlo.

Nobody from Arlo will call you about a hacked camera. Calls, texts and pop-ups like that are scams. If you let someone in, see virus and scam clean-up and read how to tell if a tech support call is a scam.

Questions

Arlo camera and doorbell FAQ

My Arlo camera says offline. What can I do?

Most offline Arlo cameras have a flat battery or have lost their connection. First check the battery level in the Arlo app and charge it if it is low. Then find out how the camera connects. Some Arlo cameras talk to a base station or SmartHub, a small box plugged into your router with a cable. Others join your Wi-Fi directly. If you have a base station or SmartHub, check that it is powered on and its cable is firmly in the router. Restart it and the router, then wait a few minutes. If it still says offline, call us and we will go through it with you.

I got a new router. Why did my Arlo cameras stop working?

It depends on how your cameras connect. If they use a base station or SmartHub, it is plugged into the router with a cable, so moving that cable to the new router and restarting it is often all it needs. If your cameras join Wi-Fi directly, each one remembers the old Wi-Fi name and password, so each one has to be joined to the new network in the Arlo app. You do not need to buy anything. We can do this with you on the phone.

Do I need the base station or SmartHub?

Some older Arlo cameras only work through a base station or SmartHub. Many newer ones can join your Wi-Fi directly, and can also use a SmartHub if you have one. A hub can help when the camera is far from the router. We look at your models with you and tell you honestly whether you need one. Do not throw the box away until we check.

Why does my Arlo battery drain so fast?

The most common reasons are a lot of motion recordings (a busy street, or trees and bushes moving in the wind), watching live video often, a weak signal, and cold weather. Batteries drain faster in the cold and may not charge at all below freezing. Bringing the battery inside to charge at room temperature helps. Fewer motion triggers and a stronger signal usually help the most.

How do I stop getting so many Arlo alerts?

In the Arlo app you can turn down the motion sensitivity, draw activity zones so the camera ignores the street, and choose which kinds of alerts you want. You can also use modes, such as armed, disarmed or a schedule, so the cameras only alert you when you want them to. Some of these options depend on your camera and Arlo plan. We set it up with you.

Do I have to pay Arlo a subscription?

Without a paid Arlo plan, most Arlo cameras still send alerts and show live video. Saving recordings to Arlo's cloud, and some smart alerts, need a paid plan. Some setups with a base station or SmartHub can save recordings to a USB drive or memory card instead. The plans and prices are set by Arlo and change from time to time, so check them in the Arlo app or on Arlo's own website. We do not sell Arlo plans and do not earn anything from them.

How do I let my son or daughter see the cameras too?

The Arlo account owner can invite family members from the Arlo app, so they can see the cameras on their own phone. Each person signs in with their own email and password, so you never need to share yours. You can remove someone at any time. We can walk you through it.

Someone called or texted asking for my Arlo verification code. Should I give it?

No. Arlo sends a code by text, email or app notification when you sign in, to prove it is really you. Never read that code to anyone who calls, texts or emails you, even if they say they are from Arlo, Netgear or your bank. We never ask for it either. If you already gave it out, change your Arlo password straight away and check who has access to your cameras. Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre in Canada or to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov in the USA.
