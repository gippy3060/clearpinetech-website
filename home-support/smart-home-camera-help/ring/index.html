Try it yourself first

Ring doorbell offline: what to check

Menus can differ a little between Ring models and app versions. If these steps do not help, call us.

  1. Check the battery. Open the Ring app and look at the battery level for the device. If it is low, charge it fully. Battery doorbells go offline when they run flat.
  2. Check Wi-Fi at the door. Stand next to the doorbell with your phone. Turn off mobile data and try to open a web page. If it is slow or will not load, the Wi-Fi does not reach the door well.
  3. Restart the router. Unplug your router (and modem, if you have a separate one) for 30 seconds, plug it back in, and wait about five minutes for the lights to settle.
  4. Did anything change? A new router, a new internet provider, or a new Wi-Fi name or password means the Ring has to be joined again. In the Ring app, open the device, go to Device Health, and choose the option to reconnect or change the Wi-Fi network.
  5. Look at the signal. Device Health also shows the Wi-Fi signal as a negative number. Closer to zero is better: -45 is strong, -70 is weak. A weak signal is the most common cause of a Ring that keeps dropping off.
  6. Have your details ready. Your Ring email address and password, and your Wi-Fi name and password. Ring will send a code to your phone or email when you sign in.
  7. Know when to stop. If the device shows no light at all after a full charge, or a wired doorbell has no power, it may be the wiring, the transformer or the device itself. That needs an electrician or Ring's own support, and we will tell you so.

Cold winters: in freezing weather, Ring batteries drain faster and may charge slowly or not at all outside. Bring the battery (or the doorbell) inside to charge at room temperature.

Wi-Fi doesn't reach the door? See Wi-Fi extender and mesh setup or Wi-Fi and router help. Other cameras and smart speakers are on our smart home and camera help page.

Good to know

Things that are specific to Ring

Device Health is your friend. In the Ring app, each device has a Device Health page. It shows the battery level, the Wi-Fi network it uses and the signal strength. This is the first place we look together.

Some Ring devices only use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Many routers send out two kinds of Wi-Fi, often called 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz. Many Ring devices can only join the 2.4 GHz one. If your router uses separate names for each, the Ring needs the 2.4 GHz name. We check this with you.

Battery or wired. Some Ring doorbells run on a battery you charge with a cable. Others are wired to your old doorbell wires, and some can do both. A wired doorbell with power problems is a job for an electrician, not a remote session.

Recordings need a paid plan. Without one, most Ring devices still send alerts and show live video, but do not save recordings to watch later. Ring sets the plans and prices, so check them in the Ring app. We do not sell Ring plans.

Two-step verification. Ring sends a code by text or email each time you sign in on a new phone. That code is only for you. Never read it to someone who calls or messages you, even if they say they are from Ring or Amazon.

Nobody from Ring or Amazon will call you about a hacked camera. Calls, texts and pop-ups like that are scams. If you let someone in, see virus and scam clean-up and read how to tell if a tech support call is a scam.

Questions

Ring doorbell and camera FAQ

My Ring doorbell says offline. What can I do?

Most offline Ring devices have lost their Wi-Fi connection or run out of battery. Check the battery level in the Ring app, and charge it if it is low. Check that Wi-Fi works on your phone standing next to the door. If you got a new router or changed your Wi-Fi password, the doorbell has to be joined to the new network: in the Ring app, open the device, go to Device Health, and choose the option to reconnect or change the Wi-Fi network. If it still will not connect, call us and we will go through it with you.

I got a new router. Why did my Ring stop working?

A Ring device remembers one Wi-Fi name and password. A new router, often from your internet provider, usually has a new name and password, so the Ring cannot find it. You do not need to buy anything. In the Ring app, open the device, go to Device Health and change the Wi-Fi network. Some newer Ring devices ask you to press a setup button on the device during this step. We can do this with you on the phone.

Why does my Ring battery drain so fast?

The most common reasons are a lot of motion alerts (a busy street or trees moving in the wind), watching Live View often, a weak Wi-Fi signal, and cold weather. Lithium batteries drain faster and charge slowly in freezing temperatures. Bringing the battery or doorbell inside to charge at room temperature helps. Fewer motion alerts and a stronger Wi-Fi signal usually help the most.

How do I check my Ring's Wi-Fi signal?

In the Ring app, open the device and tap Device Health. Look for the signal strength. It is shown as a negative number. Numbers closer to zero are better, so -45 is stronger than -70. A weak signal often means the router is too far away, and a Wi-Fi extender or mesh system near the door may help.

Do I have to pay Ring a subscription?

Without a paid Ring plan, most Ring devices still send you motion and doorbell alerts and let you see live video. To save recordings and watch them later, Ring asks for a paid plan. The plans and prices are set by Ring and change from time to time, so check them in the Ring app or on Ring's own website. We do not sell Ring plans and do not earn anything from them.

How do I let my son or daughter see the doorbell too?

The Ring account owner can invite other people as shared users in the Ring app. Each person signs in with their own email and password, so you never need to share yours. You can remove a shared user at any time. We can walk you through it.

Someone called or texted asking for my Ring verification code. Should I give it?

No. Ring sends a code by text or email when you sign in, to prove it is really you. Never read that code to anyone who calls, texts or emails you, even if they say they are from Ring, Amazon or your bank. We never ask for it either. If you already gave it out, change your Ring password straight away and check the shared users list. Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre in Canada or to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov in the USA.
