Try it yourself first

ASUS Wi-Fi slow or not working: what to check

Menus and lights differ by model, so use these as a guide. If they do not help, call us.

  1. Is it one device or all of them? If only one is slow, restart that device first.
  2. Look at the internet light. On many newer ASUS routers, a red internet (WAN) light means the router is not getting internet from your modem.
  3. Check the cable to the modem. Make sure it is pushed in firmly at both ends, and on the ASUS end it is in the internet port (often marked WAN or with a globe symbol, and often a different colour from the others).
  4. Restart in order. Unplug the modem and the ASUS router for 30 seconds. Plug in the modem and wait a few minutes. Then plug in the ASUS router and wait for its lights to settle. If you have extra AiMesh units, plug those in last.
  5. Move the router. Put it in an open, raised spot, not in a cupboard, behind the TV or on the floor. If you have a second AiMesh unit, place it about halfway between the router and the weak room, not inside the weak room.
  6. Test with a cable. If a computer plugged straight into the ASUS router is also slow, the cause may be your internet service. Your internet provider would need to check the line.
  7. Check for an update. In the ASUS Router app or the router's settings page, look for a firmware update. Leave the router plugged in until the update finishes.
  8. Be careful with resetting. Holding the small reset button erases the Wi-Fi name, password and all settings, including AiMesh. Only use it if you are ready to set everything up again, or call us first.

More general steps are on our Wi-Fi and router help page.

Good to know

Things that are specific to ASUS routers

The app or the web page. Most ASUS routers can be managed two ways: the ASUS Router app on your phone, or a settings page you open in a web browser on a computer connected to the router. Both change the same settings. We use whichever you find easier.

AiMesh. Many ASUS routers can join together as one mesh system, called AiMesh. The ZenWiFi systems are made for this. If you already own an ASUS router, adding a second compatible ASUS unit is often a good way to fix weak rooms. We check your models before you buy anything.

Lots of settings. ASUS routers have more settings than most home routers, including some for gaming (the ROG and TUF gaming models have even more). You do not need most of them. We leave anything that works alone, and explain anything we change.

Keep it updated. Security researchers have reported attacks on older, un-updated home routers from several makers, including some ASUS models. The simple protections are: install updates, change the router's sign-in password from the one it came with, and keep remote access from the internet turned off unless you really need it. If your model no longer gets updates, we will tell you plainly; whether to replace it is your decision.

Two different passwords. Your Wi-Fi password (for phones and laptops) is not the same as the router's sign-in password (for the settings). The app may also use an ASUS account. We never ask you to tell us your passwords; you type them yourself.

Behind your provider's equipment. If your internet provider gave you a modem with its own Wi-Fi, such as an Xfinity, Spectrum, Rogers or Bell box, adding an ASUS router behind it can create two networks. We can help set it up so you have one network that works well. Our pages for Xfinity, Spectrum, Rogers and Bell explain more.

Is it an ASUS laptop, not a router? See our independent help with ASUS computers. Have a different router brand? See our independent help with TP-Link, Netgear and Linksys.

Questions

ASUS router help FAQ

My ASUS Wi-Fi is slow or keeps dropping. What can I try?

Restart the modem and then the ASUS router: unplug both for 30 seconds, plug the modem in first, wait a few minutes, then plug in the ASUS router, and after that any other AiMesh units. Then test close to the router. If Wi-Fi is fine close by but weak in other rooms, the signal is not reaching, and moving the router or adding an AiMesh unit may help. If a computer on a cable is also slow, the cause may be your internet service.

What is AiMesh, and can I use it?

AiMesh is an ASUS feature that lets many ASUS routers work together as one mesh Wi-Fi system, with one Wi-Fi name for the whole home. The ZenWiFi systems are built for this, and many other ASUS routers support it too. Not every model can be added, so we check your models first and then help you pair them, usually from the ASUS Router app.

The internet light on my ASUS router is red. What does it mean?

On many newer ASUS routers, a red internet (WAN) light means the router is on but is not getting an internet connection from your modem. Check that the cable from the modem is firmly in the router's internet port, then restart the modem first and the router second. If the light stays red, the problem may be with the modem or your provider's line. Lights differ between models, so check the guide for yours or call us.

How do I keep my ASUS router secure?

Install firmware updates (newer models can do this automatically), change the router's sign-in password from the one it came with, and leave remote access from the internet turned off unless you really need it. Older models that no longer get updates will not get security fixes. We can check your settings with you and tell you honestly if your model is still supported.

Can you help me set up parental controls on my ASUS router?

Yes. Many ASUS routers have parental controls built in, such as setting times when a child's device can go online and filtering some kinds of websites. What is available depends on your model. We set it up with you so you understand how to change it later.
