Try it yourself first

AT&T Wi-Fi slow or not working: what to check

Gateways and lights differ by model, so use these as a guide. If they don't help, call us.

  1. Check the whole house. Is the problem on one device, or on all of them? If only one device is slow, restart that device first.
  2. Restart the gateway the safe way. Unplug its power cord, wait 30 seconds, and plug it back in. Give it up to 10 minutes to start up fully before you test.
  3. Don't press the reset hole. The small recessed button can put the gateway back to factory settings and erase a Wi-Fi name and password you chose. A power restart is enough.
  4. Leave the fiber cable alone. If you have AT&T Fiber, a thin cable runs from the wall to the gateway or to a small box on the wall. Don't bend it tightly or unplug it. If it was knocked loose, call AT&T.
  5. Check for an outage. The AT&T app or your AT&T account online may show a known problem in your area. An outage is for AT&T to fix. Don't pay anyone to "fix" it.
  6. Stand next to the gateway and test. If Wi-Fi is fine there but weak upstairs or at the back of the house, the signal is not reaching. Read why Wi-Fi is often slow upstairs.
  7. Give the gateway a good spot. Open, raised and central. Not in a cabinet, behind the TV, on the floor or next to a microwave or fish tank.
  8. Keep the sticker safe. The label on the gateway shows the Wi-Fi name and password, and a code for the gateway's settings. Take a photo of it, and don't read those codes to anyone who calls you.

More general steps are on our Wi-Fi and router help page.

Good to know

Things that are specific to AT&T connections

The gateway is the modem and the Wi-Fi router in one box. AT&T usually supplies one box, called a gateway, that brings in the internet and makes the Wi-Fi. When people say "the router" on an AT&T line, they normally mean this box.

Fiber or not. Some homes have AT&T Fiber; others have an older AT&T internet service over phone lines. The Wi-Fi steps are much the same, but the speeds you can expect are not. We check which you have before we start.

Your own router goes behind the gateway. On most AT&T connections you can't simply swap the gateway out for your own router. Instead, your router plugs into the gateway, and a gateway setting (often called IP Passthrough) passes the connection along. Then the gateway's own Wi-Fi is turned off, so your home has one Wi-Fi network instead of two that compete.

AT&T Internet Air is different. If your service comes over the cellular network with AT&T Internet Air, the equipment and steps are different. Tell us when you call, and we will be honest if it's outside what we can help with.

Your account is yours. We never ask for your AT&T account password. We can't change your plan, bill or service. If a screen asks for your AT&T sign-in, you type it yourself.

Questions

AT&T Wi-Fi help FAQ

My AT&T Wi-Fi is slow or keeps dropping. What can I try?

Restart the AT&T gateway: unplug its power cord, wait 30 seconds, plug it back in and give it up to 10 minutes to start up fully. Then stand near the gateway and test again. If the Wi-Fi is fine close by but weak in other rooms, the signal is not reaching, and moving the gateway or adding an extender may help. If the internet is slow even next to the gateway or with a cable plugged in, the problem may be with the service, and AT&T would need to check it.

Where do I find my AT&T Wi-Fi password?

If you never changed it, the Wi-Fi name and password are printed on a sticker on the side or bottom of the AT&T gateway. If you changed it, you can usually see or change it in the AT&T app or your AT&T account online. We can walk you through it on a call.

Should I press the reset button on my AT&T gateway?

Usually not. Unplugging the power is a safe restart. Holding in the small reset button for a while can put the gateway back to factory settings, which erases a Wi-Fi name and password you chose. Every device in the house would then need to be connected again. Try a power restart first, or call us.

Can I use my own router or mesh system with AT&T internet?

Often yes, but the AT&T gateway usually stays in place. Your own router plugs into it, and a setting on the gateway (often called IP Passthrough) hands the internet connection to your router. Then the gateway's own Wi-Fi is normally turned off so the two do not compete. We can help you set this up step by step. AT&T Internet Air uses different equipment, so check with AT&T first if you have that service.
