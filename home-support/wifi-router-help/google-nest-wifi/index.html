Try it yourself first

Nest Wifi slow or not working: what to check

Lights and app screens differ by model and change over time, so use these as a guide. If they do not help, call us.

  1. Is it one device or all of them? If only one is slow, restart that device first.
  2. Find the main router. It is the unit plugged into your modem with a cable. The others are called points. A problem on the main router affects the whole home.
  3. Check the cable to the modem. Make sure it is pushed in firmly at both ends, and that on the Nest end it is in the port marked with a globe symbol.
  4. Restart in order. Unplug the modem and the Nest Wifi for 30 seconds. Plug in the modem and wait a few minutes. Then plug in the main router, wait until its light is steady, and then plug in the points.
  5. Look at the lights. On most models, steady white means fine. A pulsing yellow or orange light usually means no internet, or a point that cannot reach the main router.
  6. Move the points. Put the main router in an open, raised spot, not in a cupboard or behind the TV. Place each point about halfway between the router and the weak room, not inside the weak room.
  7. Run a speed test. The Google Home app can test your internet speed. If the speed is low even at the main router, the cause may be your internet service, and your provider would need to check the line.
  8. Be careful with resetting. Holding the reset button erases your Wi-Fi name, password and settings. Only do it if you are ready to set everything up again, or call us first.

More general steps are on our Wi-Fi and router help page.

Good to know

Things that are specific to Google Nest Wifi

Which one do you have? Google has sold three mesh systems: Google Wifi, Google Nest Wifi, and Nest Wifi Pro. Older Google Wifi and Nest Wifi units can usually be mixed. Nest Wifi Pro only works with other Nest Wifi Pro units. On Nest Wifi (not Pro), the extra points also work as a smart speaker. We start by checking which one you have.

It all runs through your Google account. There is no separate settings page to type into a browser. Everything is in the Google Home app, signed in with the Google account that set up the Wi-Fi. If you share the home with family, they can be added to the home in the app. We never ask you to tell us your Google password or sign-in codes; you type them yourself.

Behind your provider's equipment. If your internet provider gave you a modem with its own Wi-Fi, such as an Xfinity, Spectrum, Rogers or Bell box, adding Nest Wifi behind it can create two networks. Putting the Nest itself into bridge mode turns off its extra points, so it is often the provider's box that needs changing. We can help set it up so you have one network that works well. Our pages for Xfinity, Spectrum, Rogers and Bell explain more.

Updates happen by themselves. Nest Wifi updates itself automatically, usually overnight. You do not need to install anything, and nobody needs remote access to "update" it for you.

Have a different brand? See our independent help with Linksys, TP-Link and Netgear. For Nest doorbells and cameras, see smart home and camera help.

Questions

Google Nest Wifi help FAQ

My Google Nest Wifi is slow or keeps dropping. What can I try?

Restart the modem and then the Nest Wifi: unplug both for 30 seconds, plug the modem in first and wait a few minutes, then plug in the main Nest Wifi router (the one with a cable to the modem), and after that any extra points. Then test close to the main router. If Wi-Fi is fine close by but weak in other rooms, the signal is not reaching, and moving a point may help. The Google Home app also has a speed test you can run.

What do the lights on my Nest Wifi mean?

On most Google Nest Wifi and Google Wifi units, a steady white light means everything is working. A pulsing yellow or orange light usually means the unit cannot reach the internet, or a point has lost the main router. A red light on some models points to a hardware problem that Google would need to look at. Lights differ between models, so check the guide for yours or call us.

Where did my Google Wifi app go?

Google moved Nest Wifi and Google Wifi into the Google Home app. If you used the older Google Wifi app, you may need to move your network into Google Home. You sign in with the same Google account that set up the Wi-Fi. We can walk you through it step by step.

Can I add a Nest Wifi Pro to my older Nest Wifi or Google Wifi?

Usually not. Nest Wifi Pro units only work with other Nest Wifi Pro units. Older Nest Wifi and Google Wifi units can usually be mixed with each other. If you are buying more units, we can help you check what will work with what you already have before you spend money.
