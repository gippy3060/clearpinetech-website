Try it yourself first

Fixing slow or weak Wi-Fi at home

These steps solve many cases at no cost. If they do not, call us.

  1. Restart in order. Unplug the modem and router for 30 seconds. Plug in the modem first, wait two to three minutes until its lights settle, then plug in the router.
  2. Test with a cable. Plug a computer into the router with an Ethernet cable. If the internet works by cable but not Wi-Fi, the issue is the Wi-Fi settings or signal rather than your provider.
  3. Move the router. Place it in a central, open, raised spot, away from microwaves, cordless phone bases and thick walls. Keep the antennas upright if it has them.
  4. Pick the right band. Use the 2.4 GHz network for distance and the 5 GHz network for speed at close range. Many routers combine both under one name.
  5. Check for updates. Log in to the router's admin page or app and install any firmware update. Write down the settings first in case you need to restore them.

Questions

Wi-Fi and router help FAQ

Why is my Wi-Fi slow or weak in some rooms?

Wi-Fi signal weakens with distance and with dense walls, floors, mirrors and appliances. A router tucked in a basement corner or cupboard covers less of the house. Moving it to a central, higher spot, changing the Wi-Fi channel, or adding a mesh system or extender usually helps.

Can you set up my router or mesh system remotely?

In most cases, yes. With your permission we connect to a computer on your network and configure the router through its web page or app. You may need to plug in cables or press a button on the device, and we will guide you step by step.

Do you work with Rogers, Bell, Telus, Xfinity, Spectrum or other provider routers?

Yes, we can help set up and troubleshoot the router or modem your internet provider supplied, such as changing the Wi-Fi name and password. We cannot see or change anything on your provider's side of the line. For outages or billing, contact your provider.

How much does remote Wi-Fi and router help cost?

A one-time remote support session is a flat $100 CAD, available anywhere in Canada and the United States. If we cannot fix the problem, you are not charged.

Are you affiliated with Netgear, TP-Link, Eero, Rogers, Xfinity or any other brand?

No. Clearpine Technologies is an independent support provider and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an authorised service provider of any router brand or internet provider. For free manufacturer support, contact the manufacturer directly.

Can you fix a router that has failed or a cable that is damaged?

Remote support cannot repair physical faults such as a dead router, damaged cables or an outage on your provider's network. We can tell you whether the problem looks like software settings or hardware. If we cannot fix the problem, you are not charged.
