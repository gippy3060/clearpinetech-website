A chiropractic or massage clinic lives and dies by its schedule. A chiropractor may see many short visits in a day; a massage clinic may run several treatment rooms back to back, with therapists who are independent contractors and keep their own hours. When the booking system will not load, the card terminal drops off the network or an insurer's portal locks out the front desk, the waiting room fills up in minutes.

What chiropractic and massage clinics typically need

  • A front desk that never stalls. Reliable check-in computers, a backup way to see today's schedule if the internet drops, and printers, scanners and card terminals that are monitored rather than fixed only after they fail. See the front desk section below.
  • Separate logins for every practitioner. Chiropractors, RMTs, kinesiologists and front-desk staff each get their own booking and charting account, with multi-factor authentication where the software supports it. See how to secure booking and charting software; the same steps apply to chiro and massage clinics.
  • Secure insurer and claims portals. Direct billing to extended health insurers and claims for ICBC or WorkSafeBC patients often run through online portals. Each person should have their own credentials where the portal allows it. See billing portals below.
  • X-ray images that are backed up. If your clinic takes its own X-rays, the imaging computer and image store need the same care as any other patient record. See X-ray and imaging below.
  • A clean setup for room renters and contractors. Practitioners who rent space need their own access, limited to their own clients, and that access should end on their last day. See contractors and room renters.
  • Tablets and laptops in treatment rooms. Charting at the table is convenient, but those devices need encryption, a short screen lock and a way to wipe them if they go missing.
  • Reminder emails that arrive. Appointment reminders and intake links sent from your clinic's domain should be properly authenticated so they land in the inbox rather than spam. See email security for BC clinics.
  • Backups and ransomware readiness. Patching, endpoint protection, email filtering and an offline or immutable backup copy. See five security gaps we see in clinics and cyber incident response for BC clinics.

The front desk: check-in, payments and the waiting room

In a busy chiropractic office, the front desk handles check-in, rebooking, payment and often insurer billing for every visit. Small problems there add up fast. What we usually put in place:

  • Separate networks. Card terminals, front-desk computers, treatment-room devices and patient Wi-Fi each on their own network segment, so a guest's phone never shares a network with the computer that opens patient files.
  • Check-in tablets and kiosks that are locked down. If patients fill in intake forms or check themselves in on a tablet, it should run only the check-in app, lock itself after use and never show the previous patient's details.
  • A plan for an internet outage. With cloud booking software, the internet connection is part of your clinic's equipment. Options include a backup connection, a printed or exported schedule each morning, and a paper fallback for intake and payment notes.
  • Waiting-room screens kept separate. A TV showing exercises or clinic information should not be the front-desk computer's second monitor, and should not sit on the clinical network.

Direct billing and claims portals

Many chiropractic and massage clinics bill insurers directly and submit claims for patients covered by ICBC or WorkSafeBC. Those portals hold patient, practitioner and payment information, and a compromised login can be used to submit or redirect claims. Good practice:

  • Each practitioner and staff member uses their own portal account where the portal supports it, rather than one shared clinic login on a sticky note.
  • Multi-factor authentication turned on wherever it is offered, with the second factor on a clinic-controlled device or app, not one person's personal phone that leaves with them.
  • Passwords kept in a business password manager, so a departing staff member's access can be removed and shared credentials changed the same day.
  • Remittance and payment-change notices checked by phone using a number you already have. Email requests to change where payments go are a common fraud pattern.

X-ray and imaging in chiropractic offices

Not every chiropractor takes X-rays in house, but clinics that do have a computer-based imaging system that is easy to overlook. The X-ray images are part of the patient record, and the computer connected to the imaging equipment is often older, rarely updated and set up once by the equipment installer.

  • Know where the images live. Images may be stored on the acquisition computer, a separate server, the cloud or inside your practice software. Your backup has to include wherever the originals actually are.
  • Back up the image store with an off-site copy and test a restore. The same approach we describe for dental X-ray and CBCT backups applies here: image database and files backed up together, one copy off site and protected from ransomware, and a real image restored to prove it works.
  • Coordinate updates with the equipment vendor. The imaging computer should still be protected and patched, but on a schedule the vendor supports, so an update does not stop the X-ray unit on a Monday morning.
  • Share images securely. When images go to a radiologist, another provider or a patient, use a secure sharing method rather than email attachments or USB sticks.

Contractors, associates and room renters

Massage therapists in particular often work as independent contractors or rent rooms, and many chiropractic offices bring in associates. That arrangement is normal, but it makes access control more important:

  • A written start-and-finish checklist: accounts created on day one, and booking, charting, email, Wi-Fi, door codes and portal access removed on the last day.
  • Access limited to each practitioner's own clients, unless your agreements say otherwise. Who owns and keeps the records is a question for your agreements and advisors; we handle the technical side.
  • A simple standard for personal laptops and phones used for clinic work: screen lock, encryption, current updates, no shared family accounts, and no patient information saved outside the clinic's software.
  • A quarterly review of who still has access, because practitioners come and go faster than anyone remembers to tidy up accounts.

For a fuller walk-through, including separate Wi-Fi for renters, shared printers and what to agree before someone leaves, see our guide to sharing IT with room-renting practitioners.

Mobile, event and home-visit work

Some RMTs and chiropractors treat at sports events, workplaces or patients' homes. Phones and tablets used outside the clinic should have the booking app protected by its own login, a short screen lock and remote wipe, and should use a phone's own data connection or a trusted network rather than open public Wi-Fi.

Each practitioner should have their own login with access limited to their own clients, a written standard for any personal devices used for clinic work, and access removed on their last day. Who owns and keeps the records is a question for your agreements and advisors; we handle the technical side.

Can several of us share one login for insurer billing portals?

It is best avoided. Shared logins make it impossible to see who did what and hard to remove access when someone leaves. Where a portal supports individual accounts and multi-factor authentication, use them, and keep any shared credentials in a business password manager so they can be changed quickly.

Do you support chiropractic X-ray computers?

We support the computer, network, security and backups around your imaging system, and coordinate with the X-ray equipment vendor on updates and anything specific to the imaging software. The aim is that every image is included in a tested backup with an off-site copy.
