A counselling or psychology practice holds some of the most sensitive personal information there is. Clients share things they may not have told anyone else, and they trust that it stays in the room. That trust now depends as much on laptops, passwords and video platforms as on a locked filing cabinet.

This page is for practices where counselling, psychology or therapy is the main work, because their IT looks different in a few important ways.

What makes counselling practice IT different

  • There is often no front desk and no server. Many group practices run entirely on cloud practice-management software, a video platform, email and a handful of laptops. That is simpler in some ways, but it means security lives in accounts and devices, not in a server room.
  • Clinicians work from several places. A clinician may see clients in a shared office suite on Monday, from a home office on Tuesday and from a rented room in another city on Thursday. Every one of those places needs to be private, both on the network and within earshot.
  • Many clinicians are contractors. Group practices commonly bring in associates on contract who use their own devices. Without a written standard, every laptop ends up configured differently, and nobody is sure who still has access after someone leaves.
  • The content of notes is unusually sensitive. A misdirected email or a lost unencrypted laptop in a counselling practice can do real harm to a client. The safeguards are the same as any clinic, but the cost of skipping them is higher.
  • Virtual sessions are core work, not an extra. If the video drops or the audio echoes, the session suffers. Reliable, private virtual care is a basic expectation.

What counselling and psychology practices typically need

  • Practice software that is locked down properly. Cloud platforms for booking, notes and billing are only as safe as the accounts that use them. Every clinician and admin needs their own login, multi-factor authentication, the right role (an admin who books appointments usually does not need to read session notes), and prompt removal of access when they leave. We work alongside your software vendor rather than replacing their support.
  • Private, dependable video sessions. A properly configured video platform account with MFA, waiting rooms and recording turned off unless deliberately used; a computer, camera and headset that work every time; and a connection tested for the rooms clinicians actually use. Which platforms your profession accepts is for your college or association to say; we set up the one you choose.
  • Encrypted, managed devices. Full-disk encryption, automatic screen locks, automatic updates, endpoint protection and the ability to lock or wipe a lost laptop or phone. This applies to practice-owned and, by agreement, contractor-owned devices that touch client records.
  • Safe home offices. Practice data kept off shared family computers, home Wi-Fi secured with a strong password and updated router, and a simple guide for clinicians on keeping sessions out of earshot and screens out of view. Our guide to securing a home office for BC counsellors sets it out step by step.
  • A shared-suite network that keeps you separate. Practices in shared office buildings often sit on a landlord's or neighbour's network. We set up your own firewall and Wi-Fi so your devices are not on the same network as the business next door.
  • Email that is not a privacy leak. Clear rules on what goes by ordinary email, secure sending for reports and letters, filtering for phishing and checks for mailbox takeover. Our guide to clinic email security in BC goes through it step by step.
  • Clean onboarding and offboarding. A checklist for every new clinician (accounts, MFA, device standard, practice software role) and a matching one when they leave, so access ends on their last day.
  • Backups for what lives outside the platform. Your cloud software vendor backs up their system, but scanned intake forms, letters, reports, assessment files and shared drives often sit elsewhere. We back them up and test the restore. The principles are the same as in EMR backup best practices for BC clinics.
  • Ransomware and phishing readiness. Patching, endpoint protection, MFA everywhere it is offered, and an offline or immutable backup copy. See five security gaps we see in clinics.

Counselling practice IT: common questions

Do you work with counsellors, psychologists and therapists?

Yes. We support group practices of registered clinical counsellors, psychologists, social workers, marriage and family therapists and other mental health professionals. These practices share a common shape: highly sensitive client records, cloud practice-management software, video sessions and clinicians who often work from more than one place. We scope support to how your practice actually runs.

Can you help us run private, reliable video sessions?

Yes. We set up the technical side of virtual sessions: the computer, camera, headset and internet connection, the account settings on the video platform you use, multi-factor authentication and a quiet, private room setup at the office or at home. Which platforms are acceptable for your profession is a question for your regulatory college or professional association; we help you configure the one you choose properly.

Our clinicians are independent contractors. How should IT be set up?

Usually each clinician gets their own login with multi-factor authentication, access only to their own clients' records, an encrypted device that meets a written practice standard, and a clear process for removing access when they leave. Who owns and keeps the records is a matter for your contracts and advisors; we handle the technical side and document it.

How much does managed IT support cost for a counselling practice?

Our managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. It is delivered remotely during business hours. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, although on-site visits are free if the problem cannot be fixed remotely. What privacy rules apply to a counselling practice's IT in BC?

Private practices in BC are generally subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), and your regulatory college or professional association sets expectations for client records and virtual practice. Your college, association and counsel can confirm what applies to you. We help put technical safeguards in place and document them.

Can you assess our practice on site?

Yes. Our free IT assessment is free with no obligation, and can be done on site at any location within 300 km of Abbotsford, or remotely.
