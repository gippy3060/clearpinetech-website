What clinics typically need

  • A stable environment for your EMR or practice software. Whether you run OSCAR Pro, Accuro, TELUS PS Suite, Med Access or dental and optometry practice-management software, the workstations, server (if any), network and permissions around it need to be patched, documented and fast. We work alongside your software vendor rather than replacing their support. See how your EMR's hosting model changes your IT below.
  • Privacy safeguards you can show. Unique user accounts, role-based access, encryption of laptops and servers, screen locks and audit-friendly logging. These are the technical side of your obligations under PIPA; see PIPEDA and clinic IT for how we translate them.
  • Backups that restore. Backup to a stated recovery objective, including the EMR database and imaging, with restores tested rather than assumed. See EMR backup best practices for BC clinics and why backups quietly fail.
  • Ransomware readiness. Patching, endpoint protection, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation and an offline or immutable backup copy, plus a plain-language plan for the first hour of an incident. See five security gaps we see in clinics and cyber incident response for BC clinics.
  • Secure fax and referral workflow. Many clinics still depend on fax. We help review whether e-fax or secure messaging fits your workflow and how those documents are stored and accessed. See secure e-fax for BC clinics.
  • Email that protects patient information. MFA, spoofing protection and clear rules on what should not go by ordinary email. See email security for BC clinics.
  • Imaging and diagnostic equipment. Vendor software with specific requirements and long replacement cycles has to integrate cleanly without exposing the rest of your network.
  • Front-desk reliability. Printers, card terminals, phones and Wi-Fi that work, with support that responds in clinic hours so your staff are not troubleshooting between patients.

Your EMR: on-site server, vendor-hosted or cloud

The question that shapes a clinic's IT more than any other is where the EMR actually runs. The same brand can be sold more than one way, and clinics that have been open a while sometimes are not sure which model they are on. Your vendor agreement will say; if it doesn't, the vendor's support line can tell you in a minute. Each model moves different jobs onto the clinic.

EMR on a server in the clinic. The database lives on hardware you own, usually in a back room or closet. That gives you control and keeps working through an internet outage, but the clinic carries everything the vendor would otherwise handle:

  • Operating-system and database patching on the vendor's approved schedule, so updates don't break the EMR
  • A backup of the EMR database taken the way the vendor specifies (a consistent copy, not just files copied while the database is open), plus an off-site or immutable copy that ransomware can't reach
  • Regular test restores, so you know how long getting the clinic back actually takes; see EMR backup best practices
  • A battery backup (UPS) that shuts the server down cleanly, a locked room, disk encryption and monitored disk and warranty health
  • A plan for when the hardware ages out, since a server migration has to be booked with the vendor

Vendor-hosted EMR reached by remote desktop or a client app. The vendor runs the database in their data centre and handles its server-side backups. The clinic's job shifts to the connection and the endpoints:

  • An internet connection sized for every workstation at once, and ideally a second connection from a different provider with automatic failover, because no internet now means no charts
  • Clean, patched workstations with the right client version, plus printers, label printers and scanners mapped correctly through the remote session
  • Individual logins with multi-factor authentication, and quick removal of accounts when staff or locums leave
  • Knowing what the vendor backs up, how fast they restore and how you would get a full export of your records if you ever changed vendor

Browser-based cloud EMR. Everything runs in a web browser. There is no clinic server to patch, but a stolen password now opens the whole chart from anywhere, so account and device security matter most:

  • Multi-factor authentication for every user, with no shared front-desk logins
  • Managed, encrypted devices with screen locks, and a clear rule on whether charting from personal or home computers is allowed
  • Browser hygiene: updates, no unvetted extensions, and saved passwords kept out of the browser on shared computers
  • Reliable internet and Wi-Fi in every exam room, and a paper downtime routine for outages; see our clinic incident-response guide

Whichever model you are on, the pieces around the EMR stay with the clinic: scanned documents and referral letters, e-fax, lab and specialist report delivery, email, spreadsheets and anything saved to desktops. These are also where personal health information tends to leak, because they sit outside the EMR's own access controls and audit log. Part of our assessment is finding those copies and deciding with you where they should live. See secure e-fax for BC clinics for the fax side.

Questions

Clinic IT: common questions

Can you support our EMR, such as OSCAR Pro, Accuro or TELUS PS Suite?

We support the servers or workstations, network, backup, security and user accounts around your EMR or practice-management software, and we coordinate with the vendor on application-level issues. We do not replace the vendor's own support, and where your EMR is hosted by the vendor, we work with their requirements.

Our EMR is hosted by the vendor. Do we still need IT support?

Usually, yes. A hosted or cloud EMR moves the database and its server-side backups to the vendor, but the clinic still owns the computers, user accounts, internet connection, Wi-Fi, printers, scanners, fax workflow, email and any files kept outside the EMR. Those are where most day-to-day problems and many security incidents start, and they are what we look after.

What privacy law applies to my clinic's IT in BC?

Private-sector health practices in BC are generally subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), and some organizations may also be subject to other legislation depending on how they are funded and structured. Your regulatory college and your own counsel can confirm what applies to you. We help put the technical safeguards in place and document them.

Do you work with dental offices, physiotherapists and other allied-health practices?

Yes. The IT needs of a dental office, optometry clinic, physiotherapy or chiropractic practice, counselling office or veterinary clinic are similar in shape: regulated personal information, imaging or practice software, front-desk reliability and a need for backups that actually restore.
