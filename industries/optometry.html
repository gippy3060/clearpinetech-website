The technology in an eye clinic is not exotic, but the constraints are unforgiving. A patient moves from pretest to the exam lane to the dispensary in one visit, and every stop depends on a different device talking to the same record. When the OCT cannot send its scan to the chart, or the front desk cannot open the schedule, the day backs up quickly.

What makes optometry IT different

  • Diagnostic equipment is the hard part. OCT units, fundus and retinal cameras, visual field analysers, auto-refractors and lensometers often come with their own capture computers, drivers and software versions, and vendors can be strict about what they support. Getting them to send results into your EMR, whether by DICOM or a proprietary export, takes planning and testing.
  • Images take up space. Retinal photographs and OCT scans add up year after year. Whether they live on a local server, in the equipment vendor's archive or in a cloud EMR, someone needs to know where they are, how much room is left and how they are backed up.
  • The clinic is also a store. The dispensary runs point of sale, frame inventory, lab orders and insurer claims next to clinical records. Retail and clinical systems share a network, so they need to be separated sensibly.
  • Downtime has a face. When systems are down, patients are sitting in the chair. Changes go outside clinic hours, and every change has a rollback plan.
  • Staff are not IT people. Optometrists, opticians and assistants should not be rebooting a capture station between appointments.

What optometry clinics typically need

  • A stable home for your practice software. Whether your practice-management or EMR system is installed in the clinic or hosted in the cloud, the workstations, accounts, permissions and internet connection around it need to be patched, documented and fast. We work alongside your vendor rather than replacing their support.
  • Diagnostic equipment integration. Capture stations set up to the vendor's requirements, network paths and shared folders that keep working after updates, and testing with real patient-day workflows before go-live.
  • An image archive you can trust. Storage sized for years of retinal and OCT images, with a clear answer to "if this server died tonight, how long until we could see last week's scans?" The same principles apply as in our guide to EMR backup best practices for BC clinics, and we explain why scheduled jobs often fail silently in your backups are probably not working.
  • A segmented network. Clinical devices, front-desk and dispensary computers, payment terminals and guest Wi-Fi kept on separate networks behind a business-grade firewall.
  • Patient privacy safeguards you can show. Individual logins with multi-factor authentication, role-based access, encrypted laptops and servers, screen locks in shared rooms and offboarding that actually removes access. See PIPEDA and clinic IT and clinic email security in BC.
  • Secure referrals and reports. Many optometry referrals to ophthalmologists and family physicians still go by fax. If you are moving to e-fax, read our checklist on secure e-fax for BC clinics first.
  • Ransomware readiness. Patching, endpoint protection, MFA and an offline or immutable backup copy. See five security gaps we see in clinics.
  • New builds, moves and expansions. Cabling for every lane, pretest station and dispensary position, an equipment list, imaging integration and a cut-over schedule. Our guide to what it costs to set up IT for a new optometry clinic walks through a line-item budget.

Questions

Optometry clinic IT: common questions

Can you support our optometry practice-management or EMR software?

We support the server or workstations, network, backups, security and user accounts around your practice-management or EMR software, whether it is hosted in the clinic or in the cloud, and we coordinate with the software vendor on application-level issues. We do not replace the vendor's own support, and we work within their system requirements.

Can you connect our OCT, fundus camera and other diagnostic equipment to our EMR?

Yes, that is a core part of clinic work for us. We handle the capture computers, network, storage and permissions that diagnostic devices depend on, and work with the equipment and software vendors on the export or integration method they support. Diagnostic equipment often has strict vendor requirements, so we review them before changing anything.

How much does managed IT support cost for an optometry clinic?

Our managed IT support is $95 per user per month, with a minimum of 5 users and a 1-year agreement. It is delivered remotely during business hours. Microsoft 365 licences, after-hours support and on-site visits are billed separately, although on-site visits are free if the problem cannot be fixed remotely. One-time costs for a new clinic build are covered in our optometry clinic setup cost guide.

What privacy rules apply to an optometry clinic's IT in BC?

Private optometry practices in BC are generally subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), and the College of Optometrists of BC sets professional expectations for patient records. Your college, professional association and counsel can confirm what applies to you. We help put technical safeguards in place and document them.

Can you assess our clinic on site?

Yes. Our free IT assessment is free with no obligation, and can be done on site at any location within 300 km of Abbotsford, or remotely.
